# On the coefficients of Coxeter polynomials of trees and bipartite quivers

**arXiv:** [2509.02375](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02375) · N. Harel, S. Ladkani ·
2 Sep 2025 · *read 2026-09-19 from the arXiv PDF*

Eleven pages, one formula, and it answers a question H-015 asks: *is there an
invariant that counts relations against cords?* Yes — the **second coefficient of
the Coxeter polynomial**, and it is already sitting in `coxeterKey`.

The paper's own contribution is a spectral-graph-theory proof (via A'Campo) of a
result of Happel's, extended to bipartite quivers. We use the tree specialisation.

## The result we use

### Theorem 1.1

> Let `Q` be a bipartite quiver on `n ≥ 2` vertices without parallel arrows, with
> `e` arrows, and let `q = Σ_{i<j} C(|B_i ∩ B_j|, 2)` count the `Ã_{2,2}` full
> subquivers. Write `φ_Q(x) = x^n + a_1 x^{n-1} + a_2 x^{n-2} + …`. Then
>
>     a_1 = n - e
>     a_2 = (n-e)(n-1-e)/2 + e - 2q - Σ_{i=1}^{n} C(d_i, 2)
>
> where `d_i` is the degree of vertex `i` in the underlying graph.

**For a tree** (`e = n-1`, `q = 0`, and every tree has a bipartite orientation,
whose derived class is the tree's) this collapses to Happel's statement

    a_1 = 1        a_2 = (n - 1) - Σ_i C(d_i, 2).

### The quipu corollary (ours, from the above)

For a tree with **maximum degree ≤ 3** — so for every quipu — let `n_1, n_2, n_3`
be the numbers of vertices of each degree. From `n_1+n_2+n_3 = n` and
`Σ d_i = 2n-2` one gets `n_2 + 2n_3 = n-2` and `n_3 = n_1 - 2`, hence
`Σ C(d_i,2) = n_2 + 3n_3 = n + n_1 - 4`. Therefore

> **For any tree of maximum degree ≤ 3 on `n` vertices,
> `a_2 = 3 - (number of leaves)`.**

Checked against `nakayama.QuipuAlgebra` for **every** quipu of order 4 to 12: no
exceptions, and `a_1 = 1` throughout.

Since an open quipu has one leaf per cord of positive length plus the two ends of
the main string, this reads: **`number of cords = 1 - a_2`**.

### What it says about an LNA

By `thm:QuipuToAn`, an LNA with almost separate relations is derived equivalent to
the quipu whose cords are its relations of `l_i ≥ 3` arrows (a relation of length
2 gives `m_i = 0`, no cord). Writing `c = #{i : l_i ≥ 3}`, the corollary gives

> **`a_2(Λ) = 1 - c` for every LNA `Λ` with almost separate relations.**

Checked for `5 ≤ n ≤ 11`: it holds for **all** of them — and, unexpectedly, for
no others:

| `n` | LNAs with almost separate relations | LNAs with `a_2 = 1 - c` | agree |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 13 | 13 | yes |
| 6 | 34 | 34 | yes |
| 7 | 89 | 89 | yes |
| 8 | 233 | 233 | yes |
| 9 | 610 | 610 | yes |
| 10 | 1597 | 1597 | yes |
| 11 | 4181 | 4181 | yes |

(the counts are the even-index Fibonacci numbers, which is a separate fact about
how many LNAs are almost separate). For the rest, `a_2 > 1 - c` always — the
excess `d = a_2 + c - 1` is a non-negative integer, `0` exactly on the
almost-separate ones. Forward direction is a theorem; the converse is an
observation at these lengths, not a proof.

**So `a_2` is the cord counter.** Given any LNA, `1 - a_2` is the number of cords
of any quipu in its class, computed in `O(n^3)` and with no search — and if the
class contains an almost-separate LNA, that LNA has exactly `1 - a_2` relations
of length `≥ 3`. Two LNAs with different `a_2` are in different classes (which
the Coxeter polynomial already said), but the point is the *reading*: `a_2` is
not just a number, it is the cord count.

## Lemmas worth knowing

- The `q` term is the only place the formula is not local: it counts full
  subquivers of type `Ã_{2,2}`, which can only occur with two vertices having two
  common neighbours. Trees have none, so the term vanishes for us; but it is the
  reason the formula does not extend naively to a quiver with a cycle.
- The paper's route is A'Campo's theorem relating the Coxeter polynomial of a
  bipartite quiver to the characteristic polynomial of the adjacency matrix of
  the underlying graph. That is the same identity `quipuForms.adjacencySpectrum-
  Polynomial` relies on, and the same one that makes F-010 true (the Coxeter
  polynomial of a tree algebra is determined by the graph spectrum).
- The first coefficient `a_1 = n - e` has a Hochschild-cohomology reading (the
  paper's references [9], [10, Remark 4.7]): it counts paths parallel to arrows.
  For a tree it is 1 and says nothing.

## Caveats and limitations

- **The theorem is about hereditary algebras only.** `φ_Q` is the Coxeter
  polynomial of a path algebra; there are no relations anywhere in the statement.
  Everything it says about an LNA comes through `thm:QuipuToAn`, so it only
  speaks about LNAs that *are* derived equivalent to a tree algebra.
- **It is not new mathematics.** The tree case is Happel's, reproved; the paper's
  own novelty is the bipartite generalisation and the spectral proof. Cite Happel
  if it matters.
- **`a_2` does not identify the quipu**, only its leaf count. Two cospectral
  quipus have the same `a_2` by definition — they have the same *whole*
  polynomial. For separating those, see
  `math-0610685-sheaves-over-finite-posets.md`.
- **Bipartite, no parallel arrows.** Since we now permit parallel arrows in
  mutated quivers (F-039), do not apply Theorem 1.1 to one.
- The converse observation (`a_2 = 1-c ⟹ almost separate`) is unproved and was
  only checked to `n = 11`. Do not use it as a test for almost-separateness in
  code without either a proof or a much wider check; `hasAlmostSeparateRelations`
  is O(n) anyway.

## What it does not give us

- No derived equivalence of any kind.
- No separation beyond what the full Coxeter polynomial already gives — `a_2` is
  one of its coefficients.
- Nothing about the third coefficient, which is where a finer structural reading
  would have to live.

## How it lands on our problem

H-015 wants "an invariant that counts relations against cords" and guesses the
Euler form. The answer is one coefficient of a polynomial the repo already
computes: `coxeterKey(Λ)[n-2] = 1 - (number of cords)`. Two immediate uses:

1. **A prefilter on the quipu search.** Before asking whether an LNA is derived
   equivalent to a given quipu, check that the quipu has `1 - a_2` cords. This
   prunes `quipuForms` lookups to a single cord count.
2. **A sharpened form of H-015.** The quantity to study is the excess
   `d(Λ) = a_2(Λ) + c(Λ) - 1`, which is `0` exactly on the almost-separate LNAs
   at `n ≤ 11` and positive elsewhere. It is *not* the obvious overlap count —
   comparing it against `Σ max(0, overlap - 1)` over consecutive (or all) pairs
   of relations fails for 1581 of the 4862 LNAs at `n = 10` — so what `d` counts
   is an open question, and a good one: it is a derived-invariant-minus-a-
   combinatorial-count that vanishes exactly on the case the quipu theorem covers.
