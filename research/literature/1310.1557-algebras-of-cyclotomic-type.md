# Algebras whose Coxeter polynomials are products of cyclotomic polynomials

**arXiv:** [1310.1557](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1557) · J. A. de la Peña ·
6 Oct 2013 · *read 2026-09-19 from the arXiv PDF*

The reference for **periodicity of the Coxeter transformation**: what it means,
how to read it off the Coxeter polynomial we already compute, and what it forces
about the derived category. This is where Lenzing–Meltzer–Ruan's "green wall"
argument comes from, so it is the paper to cite if we implement that certificate.

Standing setting: `k` algebraically closed, `A` **triangular** (quiver without
oriented cycles — every LNA qualifies), hence `gl.dim A < ∞`. `φ_A = −C^{-T}C` and
`χ_A = det(T·I − φ_A)`, our convention.

`A` is of **cyclotomic type** when `χ_A` is a product of cyclotomic polynomials.

## Results we use

### Theorem 1 — six equivalent readings of "cyclotomic type"

> For `A` triangular, these are equivalent: (1) `|λ_i| ≤ 1` for all roots;
> (2) `|λ_i| = 1` for all roots; (3) spectral radius `ρ_A = 1`; (4) Mahler measure
> `M(φ_A) = 1`; (5) energy `e(φ_A) = n`; (6) `χ_A` factors into cyclotomic
> polynomials.

(6) is the one to test; the rest are free consequences. Kronecker's theorem is what
makes them equivalent. Note `e(φ_A) ≥ n` always (his §2), so (5) is an extremal
condition.

### §2.10 Theorem — periodicity, and the **period**

> The following are equivalent for `A`:
> 1. `φ_A` is periodic;
> 2. `A` is of cyclotomic type **and** `φ_A` is diagonalizable;
> 3. writing `χ_A = ∏_{m∈M} Φ_m^{e(m)}`, `φ_A` has period `lcm{ φ(m) : m ∈ M }`.

> ⚠ **(3) is misprinted.** `Φ_m` has *`φ(m)` roots*, each a *primitive `m`-th* root
> of unity, and the paper's §2.2 states this the wrong way round ("the roots of
> `Φ_n` are primitive `φ(n)`-roots of unity"), which propagates into (3) and into
> Theorem 2(c),(d). **The correct period is `lcm{ m : m ∈ M }`.** Checked here
> 2026-09-19: `N_15(5)` has `χ = Φ_2³ Φ_8 Φ_10²`, so `lcm{2,8,10} = 40`, and the
> order of `φ` computed directly is **40**; `lcm{φ(2),φ(8),φ(10)} = 4` is wrong.
> The paper's own Dynkin table agrees with the corrected rule (`A_n`:
> `χ = ∏_{d | n+1, d>1} Φ_d`, Coxeter number `n+1`).

Cyclotomic type is **necessary but not sufficient** for periodicity — the Jordan
blocks must be trivial. §6.1 says so explicitly, citing Ladkani [20].

### Theorem 2 — what periodicity forces about the derived category

Let `Γ_A` be the AR quiver of `Der(A) = D^b(mod A)` and `[Γ_A]` the *class quiver*
(objects identified when equal in `K_0`).

> (a) If `A` has the fractional Calabi–Yau property then `φ_A` is periodic.
> (b) **Every component of `[Γ_A]` is a tube if and only if `φ_A` is periodic.**
> If `φ_A` is periodic then `A` is of cyclotomic type, and
> (c) the period is `lcm{m : m ∈ M}` [corrected, as above];
> (d) every component of `Γ_A` is either a tube `ZA_∞/(p)` of finite period
> `p = m` for some `m ∈ M` [corrected], or of the form `Z∆` with `∆` Dynkin or
> extended Dynkin, or of one of the shapes `A_∞`, `A_∞^∞`, `D_∞`;
> (e) if `p_1, …, p_s` are the periods `> 1` of the non-homogeneous tubes in `[Γ]`
> then `Σ_i p_i ≤ n`.

Part (d) is the machinery behind Lenzing–Meltzer–Ruan's Proposition 4.3 and the
green-wall argument: piecewise hereditary categories have components of shape
`Z∆` with `∆` finite (module type) or tubes (sheaf type), and the allowed tube
ranks for a **weight triple** are exactly 3, 4, 6. So a periodic `φ_A` with period
outside what those shapes allow rules out piecewise heredity.

**The certificate, assembled** (this is the form to implement; it is not stated as
a theorem anywhere, it is assembled from Thm 2 here + LMR Prop. 4.3 + Prop. 1.4 of
arXiv:0805.1018):

1. factor `χ_A` into cyclotomics; if it does not factor, stop — `A` is not of
   cyclotomic type and this says nothing;
2. check `φ_A^m = I` for `m = lcm` of the indices; if not, stop (non-trivial Jordan
   blocks — cyclotomic does not imply periodic);
3. `A` is now periodic of order `m`. If `A` were piecewise hereditary it would be
   either
   - of **module type**, and then derived equivalent to `k∆` with `∆` **Dynkin** on
     `n` vertices (extended Dynkin gives a Jordan block, wild gives `ρ > 1`), so
     `χ_A` would have to be *exactly* the Coxeter polynomial of `A_n` or `D_n`, or
     of `E_6/E_7/E_8` when `n = 6,7,8`; or
   - of **sheaf type**, and then tubular, so `m ∈ {2,3,4,6}` (the tubular weight
     types `(2,2,2,2), (3,3,3), (2,4,4), (2,3,6)`; Lenzing–Meltzer–Ruan state
     `{3,4,6}`, for weight *triples* only — use `{2,3,4,6}` to be safe).

   If neither holds, **`A` is not piecewise hereditary**, hence in no quipu class.

Verified here 2026-09-19 on the Lenzing–Meltzer–Ruan wall members: `N_16(3)`
(period 18; `A_16` would need 17, `D_16` 30), `N_15(4)` and `N_15(6)` (12),
`N_15(5)` (40), `N_14(7)` (126), `N_17(8)` (24) — all six certified
non-piecewise-hereditary, all beyond what `failsFlankedPairCriterion` and
`failsBigOverlapCriterion` reach.

### §2.3 Proposition — `χ` is complete for representation-finite hereditary algebras

> For `A` connected hereditary representation-finite over `k` algebraically closed,
> `χ_A` determines `A` up to derived equivalence.

Same statement as Proposition 1.4 of arXiv:0805.1018. The table of Dynkin Coxeter
polynomials and numbers is given in full (`A_n`: `n+1`; `D_n`: `2(n−1)`; `E_6`:
12; `E_7`: 18; `E_8`: 30), which is a handy lookup when a computed `χ` turns out
to be one of them. Consequence for F-010: a cospectral **quipu** group cannot
contain a Dynkin quipu.

### §2.6 — the homological quadratic form

`h_A(x) = x^T(C_A^{-1} + C_A^{-T})x` is the symmetrization of the inverse Cartan
matrix; `⟨u,v⟩ = u^T C_A^{-1} v` is the Euler form, with
`⟨dim X, dim Y⟩ = Σ_j (−1)^j dim_k Ext^j(X,Y)` and `⟨x, φ_A y⟩ = −⟨y, x⟩`.

The paper states, citing [33] (de la Peña, *The Tits form of a tame algebra*):

> `h_A` is positive definite (resp. non-negative of corank 1) if and only if `A` is
> derived equivalent to `k∆` with `∆` of Dynkin (resp. extended Dynkin) type.

> ⚠ **Do not implement the converse.** §6.2 of this same paper exhibits, for every
> `n ≥ 7`, algebras `A_n` (commutativity relation) and `B_n` (long path zero) on
> one quiver `Q_n` with **`h_{A_n} = h_{B_n}` positive definite, `A_n`
> representation-finite and `B_n` wild**. A wild algebra is not derived equivalent
> to a Dynkin path algebra, so the "if" direction of the quoted statement cannot
> hold as literally written — [33] is about tame algebras and the hypothesis has
> been dropped in the restatement.
>
> The **safe direction** is the trivial one: the Euler form is a derived invariant
> up to `Z`-congruence, so derived equivalent to Dynkin `⟹ h_A` positive definite.
> Use it contrapositively: **`h_A` not positive definite ⟹ not derived equivalent
> to a Dynkin quiver**, i.e. not in a Dynkin quipu class. Likewise `h_A` not
> non-negative ⟹ not in an extended-Dynkin class.

§6.2 is also worth keeping for its own sake: **the homological form is not a
complete derived invariant either**, and it can fail to separate a
representation-finite algebra from a wild one.

### §6.1 Theorem — the form controls the spectrum

> If `h_A` is non-negative then `A` is of cyclotomic type. If `h_A` is positive
> definite then `φ_A` is diagonalizable and periodic.

The proof is short and self-contained (a perturbation `C_ε = C_A Σ(−1)^i(εC_A)^i`
making `h_ε` positive definite). Useful as a shortcut: positive-definite form ⟹
periodic, so step 2 of the certificate above can be skipped.

### Intro, item (3) — an LNA statement in passing

> The quotient algebras `A_n(3)` of the linear quiver `1 → 2 → … → n` with relations
> `x³ = 0` are of cyclotomic type, **for `n` even and many instances of `n` odd**.

`A_n(3)` is `N_n(3)`, our `3^(n−3) 0`. Compare arXiv:0805.1018 Prop. 5.8, which
names the derived type for `n ≤ 12`. The hedge "many instances of `n` odd" is the
paper's; it is not a theorem for all `n`.

## Lemmas worth knowing

- **§2.6 Lemma.** If `u, v` are eigenvectors of `φ_A` with eigenvalues `λ ≠ μ^{-1}`
  then `⟨u,v⟩ = 0`; and if `λ ≠ −1` then `h_A(u) = 0`. The bridge between the
  spectrum and the form.
- **§2.9 Lemma.** If `D^b(A)` is `p/q`-Calabi–Yau then `σ^{2q} = 1` for the Serre
  functor's action on `K_0` and `φ^{2q} = 1`. So the fractional CY property is a
  sufficient condition for periodicity with an explicit bound.
- **§5 Theorem** (Dynkin-class supercanonical algebras): `φ_A` periodic ⟺
  `φ_A^p = 1` with `p = lcm` of the periods of the arms ⟺ `h_A` non-negative with
  `rad h_A` of rank two ⟺ `h_B` non-negative with `rad h_B` of rank one, where
  `B = A/(α)` deletes the source. The only place in the paper where periodicity is
  *decided* combinatorially rather than computed.

## Caveats and limitations

- **The `φ(m)` / `m` misprint** (above) infects Theorem 2(c),(d) and §2.2. It is
  the single thing most likely to mislead a reader of this paper.
- **§2.6 as restated is false** — see the warning. Only the contrapositive is safe.
- Cyclotomic type ⇏ periodic (Jordan blocks), and periodic ⇏ piecewise hereditary;
  the implications only run one way. Nothing here certifies that an algebra **is**
  piecewise hereditary.
- Everything needs `A` triangular and `k` algebraically closed. LNAs are
  triangular; the field hypothesis is a real restriction we inherit.
- The AR-component results (Thm 2(d),(e)) are about the derived category's AR
  quiver, which we do not compute. They are useful as the *justification* of the
  green-wall certificate, not as something to run.

## What it does not give us

- No merge of any kind.
- No invariant beyond `χ_A` and `h_A`, and §6.2 shows `h_A` is not complete either.
- No statement specific to Nakayama algebras except the hedged remark about
  `N_n(3)`.

## How it lands on our problem

Two things, both new to us:

1. **A cheap non-piecewise-hereditary certificate from a polynomial we already
   have.** Factor `χ_A` into cyclotomics, check `φ_A^m = I`, and if `χ_A` is not a
   Dynkin Coxeter polynomial of the right degree and `m ∉ {2,3,4,6}`, the algebra
   is in no quipu class and not of sheaf type either. This is independent of
   `failsFlankedPairCriterion` and `failsBigOverlapCriterion`, and it fires where
   they do not — it certified all six of Lenzing–Meltzer–Ruan's wall members here.
2. **A second, cheap necessary condition for a quipu class:** if the quipu is
   Dynkin, `h_A` must be positive definite; if extended Dynkin, non-negative of
   corank 1. Contrapositively this rules a merge out in `O(n³)`. But **do not**
   use it as a sufficient condition — §6.2 is the counterexample.
