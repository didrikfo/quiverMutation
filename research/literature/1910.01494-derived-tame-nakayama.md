# Derived tame Nakayama algebras

**arXiv:** [1910.01494](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01494) · V. Bekkert, H. Giraldo,
J. A. Vélez-Marulanda · 1 Oct 2019 · *read 2026-09-19 from the arXiv PDF, all 15 pages*

Read to answer one question: the abstract says a derived tame Nakayama algebra
**without simple projective module** is gentle or derived equivalent to some
**skewed-gentle** algebra. If that covered LNAs, a derived invariant of
skewed-gentle algebras would transport to them along the equivalence and the
Avella-Alaminos–Geiß line the README dropped (R-008) would reopen.

**It does not cover LNAs.** "Without simple projective module" means *cycle*
algebra, and every LNA has a simple projective. The answer to the question is
**no, the AG line does not reopen** — see "Does this reopen AG?" below.

But the paper is still worth a file, for a reason that has nothing to do with its
own theorem: **its line-algebra case is a pointer to Brüstle's classification of
derived-tame tree algebras**, which is a complete merging criterion for a large
family of LNAs. That is written up separately in
[bruestle-derived-tame-tree-algebras.md](bruestle-derived-tame-tree-algebras.md),
and it is the part of this reading that matters.

## Setting and notation

`k` algebraically closed, arbitrary characteristic. A connected basic Nakayama
algebra with `n` simples is `kQ/I` with `Q` one of

- `L_n : 1 →a_1→ 2 →a_2→ ⋯ →a_{n-1}→ n` — a **line algebra**;
- `C_n : 0 →a_0→ 1 → ⋯ → n-1 →a_{n-1}→ 0` — a **cycle algebra**;

`I` admissible, `R_A` its minimal generating set of paths, a path of `m` arrows
called a *minimal `m`-relation*. **An LNA is exactly a line algebra** in this
terminology, and `R_A` is exactly our relation set (our digit `d` at position `i`
is a minimal `d`-relation starting at vertex `i`).

## The results, with full hypotheses

### Definition 1.1 — the class `D`

`A = kQ/I` finite dimensional, basic, connected Nakayama, is in `D` when

- **(C1)** `I` is generated by paths of length **two or three**;
- **(C2)** if `a_i a_{i+1} a_{i+2} ∈ R_A` then `a_{i+1}a_{i+2}a_{i+3} ∈ R_A` or
  `a_{i-1}a_i a_{i+1} ∈ R_A`;
- **(C3)** if `a_i a_{i+1} a_{i+2}` and `a_{i+1}a_{i+2}a_{i+3}` are both in `R_A`
  then `a_{i-1}a_i a_{i+1} ∉ R_A` and `a_{i+2}a_{i+3}a_{i+4} ∉ R_A`.

A 3-relation violating (C2) is called **isolated**. In digit strings: digits lie
in `{0,2,3}`, and the positions carrying a `3` form a disjoint union of blocks of
**exactly two** consecutive positions. (For a line the missing neighbours at the
ends simply make the corresponding disjunct false.)

### Theorem 1.2 — the derived representation type

> `A` basic connected Nakayama. `A` is **derived tame** iff one of
> (i) `A` is a line algebra and its Euler form is **non-negative**;
> (ii) `A` belongs to `D`.

Derived tame is Definition 2.1 (Bekkert–Drozd): a set of rational families of
bounded minimal projective complexes covering all but finitely many
indecomposables of each vector rank. Derived tameness is preserved under derived
equivalence (Theorem 2.8, from Geiß–Krause).

**The proof of (i) is a citation, not an argument.** §3.3, first line: *"If `A` is
a line algebra then in this situation Theorem 1.2 follows from [10, Thm 1.1] (see
also [20])"* — [10] is Brüstle, *Derived-tame tree algebras*, [20] is Geiß,
*Derived tame algebras and Euler forms*. **Everything this paper gives us about
LNAs is Brüstle's, and Brüstle gives strictly more (the full classification, not
just the tame/wild split).** Read the Brüstle file, not this one, for the usable
statement.

### Theorem 1.3 — the gentle / skewed-gentle dichotomy

> Let `A` be a **cycle algebra**. Then `A` is derived tame iff `A` is gentle or
> derived equivalent to some skewed-gentle algebra.

The hypothesis in the body is **"cycle algebra"**, flatly. The abstract's phrase
"without simple projective module" **occurs nowhere else in the paper** — I
grepped the full text; it appears in the abstract and in no theorem, lemma,
proof or remark. The two are meant to be the same condition, and they are (next
section), but the paper never says so.

### Proposition 3.9 — the construction behind Theorem 1.3

> `A ∈ D` and not gentle ⟹ `A` derived equivalent to the skewed-gentle algebra
> `A_Ω`.

Concretely, with `Ω = {i : a_{i-2}a_{i-1}a_i, a_{i-1}a_ia_{i+1} ∈ R_A}` (the
middle vertex of each consecutive 3-relation pair) and `Sp = {i : i-1 ∈ Ω}`:
contract each `i ∈ Ω` (replace `i-1 → i → i+1` by a single arrow `b_{i-1}`, and
the relation pair `a_{i-2}a_{i-1}a_i, a_{i-1}a_ia_{i+1}` by `a_{i-2}b_{i-1},
b_{i-1}a_{i+1}`) to get a **gentle** algebra `A_ω`; then `(Q_{A_ω}, Sp, R_{A_ω})`
is a skewed-gentle triple and `A_Ω` is the associated skewed-gentle algebra. The
tilting complex is explicit: `T_i : 0 → A_i → 0` for `i+1 ∉ Ω`, and
`T_i : 0 → A_i → A_{i+1} → 0` in degrees 1, 0 for `i+1 ∈ Ω`.

**This proposition is stated for all of `D`, not only cycle algebras.** That is
the one place the paper could have reached line algebras — see the caveat below
for why it still does not help.

### Corollary 1.4 / 1.5 — singularity category; **do not port 1.5 to LNAs**

> 1.4: `A` a derived tame **cycle** algebra ⟹ `D_sg(A) ≅ D^b(k-mod)/[|R_A|]`.
> 1.5: `A`, `B` derived equivalent derived tame **cycle** algebras ⟹ `|R_A| = |R_B|`.

Corollary 1.5 reads like "the number of relations is a derived invariant" and it
is **false for line algebras**. `kA_4` (digit string `00`, no relations) and
`kA_4/(a_1a_2)` (`20`, one relation) have the same Brüstle invariant — corank 0,
Dynkin type `A_4` — hence are derived equivalent (Brüstle Thm 1.2), with
`|R_A| = 0 ≠ 1 = |R_B|`. This is consistent with `corollary:lengthtworelations`
of arXiv:2310.08346 and with our own double mutation, which adds a relation.
An LNA has finite global dimension, so `D_sg = 0` and Corollary 1.4 is vacuous
for us; 1.5 depends on 1.4 and dies with it.

## Does an LNA have a simple projective module? Yes — and the reason

The hypothesis is the whole ballgame, so here is the argument rather than an
assertion.

For a Nakayama algebra `kQ/I` the indecomposable projective at vertex `i` has as
basis the paths starting at `i` that are nonzero mod `I` (in the opposite module
convention, the paths *ending* at `i`; the argument is the same read backwards).
So `P_i` is simple exactly when no arrow survives out of `i`, and since `I` is
admissible, `I ⊆ rad²`, so no single arrow lies in `I`. Therefore

> `P_i` is simple ⟺ no arrow of `Q` starts at `i`.

- **Line `L_n`:** vertex `n` is a sink — no arrow starts there. So `P_n = S_n` is
  a **simple projective**, for *every* admissible `I`, whatever the relation
  lengths. The linear orientation `1 → 2 → ⋯ → n` guarantees an endpoint with no
  outgoing arrow, and that endpoint is the simple projective. (Dually, in the
  other convention, vertex `1` gives it.) **Every LNA has a simple projective.**
- **Cycle `C_n`:** every vertex has an outgoing arrow `a_i ∉ I`, so every `P_i`
  has length ≥ 2 and there is **no** simple projective.

So "Nakayama algebra without simple projective module" = "cycle algebra"
exactly, which is why the abstract and Theorem 1.3 say the same thing. **LNAs are
excluded from Theorem 1.3 by its hypothesis, categorically and with no border
cases.** That is the finding.

## Caveats and limitations

- **The abstract oversells the reach.** Read alone, "a derived tame Nakayama
  algebra without simple projective module is gentle or derived equivalent to
  some skewed-gentle algebra" sounds like a statement about Nakayama algebras in
  general. It is a statement about cycle algebras, i.e. about the half of the
  Nakayama world we do not work in.
- **Theorem 1.2 (ii) looks like it might give line algebras a second route to
  tameness, and does not.** The disjunction "(i) line with non-negative Euler
  form **or** (ii) in `D`" would be sharp only if some line algebra in `D` had an
  indefinite Euler form — which would contradict Brüstle. **Checked: none does.**
  I enumerated every LNA for `3 ≤ n ≤ 13` (2, 5, 14, …, 208012 — the Catalan
  numbers `C_{n-1}`, and 1430 at `n = 9` as arXiv:2310.08346 says), and in every
  size the line algebras in `D` are a proper subset of those with non-negative
  Euler form (`n = 9`: 214 in `D`, 697 non-negative). So (ii) is **redundant for
  line algebras** and the theorem is consistent; but nothing in the paper says
  this, and a reader could easily take (ii) for extra content.
- **Proposition 3.9 does apply to line algebras in `D`, and still gives nothing.**
  It is stated for `A ∈ D` without restricting to cycles, so a line algebra in
  `D` and not gentle *is* derived equivalent to the skewed-gentle `A_Ω`. But a
  line algebra in `D` has relations of length 2 and 3 only, with the 3-relations
  in consecutive pairs — a narrow family (214 of the 1430 at `n = 9`) that
  Brüstle already classifies completely and unconditionally. The skewed-gentle
  detour adds no separating power over Brüstle's `(n, corank, Dynkin type)`.
- **Two different definitions of "derived tame" are in play.** Brüstle defines it
  as tameness of the repetitive category `Â`; this paper uses the
  Bekkert–Drozd/Geiß–Krause parametrising-set definition (Definition 2.1). They
  are believed to coincide for finite global dimension and the paper treats them
  as interchangeable when it cites [10]. **I did not verify that equivalence.**
- Algebraically closed field throughout, as usual, and as usual that is a real
  hypothesis for us (cf. the Happel–Seidel file).

## What it does not give us

- No statement about **line** algebras that is not Brüstle's.
- No derived invariant. Theorem 1.3 produces an equivalence, not a separator;
  Corollary 1.5 is the only invariant in the paper and it is confined to cycles.
- No classification of derived **wild** Nakayama algebras, which for LNAs is the
  large and hard part (at `n = 13`, 200651 of 208012 LNAs are derived wild).
- Nothing on relation lengths ≥ 4 except via Lemma 3.5, which is again cyclic:
  *a derived tame cycle algebra has `I` generated by relations of length two or
  three*. There is **no** line analogue — `3033030` has three-arrow relations and
  is derived tame, and `kA_9/rad^8` (`80000000`… in the obvious sense) has an
  eight-arrow relation and is derived tame too.

## Lemmas worth knowing (all cyclic, recorded for completeness)

- **Lemma 3.2.** The cycle truncated algebra `C(n,r) = kC_n/(all paths of length
  r)` is derived tame **iff `r = 2`**. Via self-injectivity: [3, Cor. 2.5] says a
  self-injective algebra is derived discrete or derived wild, and `C(n,r)` is not
  derived discrete for `r ≥ 3`. *Note the contrast with the line:* `kA_n/rad^r`
  is derived tame for a range of `r` (Happel–Seidel territory). Self-injectivity
  is what kills the cycle, and line algebras are never self-injective.
- **Lemma 3.1** [6, Lemma 3.1]. `B = eAe` a full subalgebra of `A`, `B` derived
  wild ⟹ `A` derived wild. This is the engine of every wildness proof here, and
  it is the cyclic cousin of our `removevertex` corollary — **but note it runs the
  other way**: `removevertex` propagates *non-piecewise-heredity* upward from a
  vertex deletion; this propagates *derived wildness* upward from an idempotent
  subalgebra. Both are "certify the small one, conclude about the big one".
- **Lemmas 3.3, 3.4.** An isolated 3-relation, or three consecutive 3-relations,
  in a cycle algebra ⟹ derived wild.

## Does this reopen the Avella-Alaminos–Geiß line? **No.**

Stated as plainly as the brief asks for:

1. **The hypothesis excludes every LNA.** Theorem 1.3 requires no simple
   projective module; every LNA has one (argument above); so the theorem never
   applies to an LNA and transports no invariant to one.
2. **The one clause that does reach line algebras (Proposition 3.9) reaches only
   line algebras in `D`** — relations of length 2 and 3, 3-relations in
   consecutive pairs — and those are already completely classified by Brüstle's
   `(n, corank, Dynkin type)`. An AG-type invariant of skewed-gentle algebras
   could at best reproduce a classification we would then already have, for a
   family that is a small minority of LNAs (214/1430 at `n = 9`) and shrinking.
3. **The original grounds for R-008 are untouched.** An LNA with a relation of
   three or more arrows is still not gentle, and the gentle LNAs are still one
   derived class (F-019, and arXiv:2608.08222 Theorem 1.5 in this directory says
   it again). Nothing here gives an LNA with a long relation a gentle or
   skewed-gentle model in a way that separates anything.

So **do not go looking for the skewed-gentle derived invariant** (Bekkert–Marcos–
Merklen [8], or Avella-Alaminos' extension) on the strength of this paper. If
that line is ever reopened it will have to be by a paper that puts a *line*
Nakayama algebra with long relations into the skewed-gentle world, and this is
not that paper. R-008 stands.

## How it applies to LNAs — the one thing to take away

Theorem 1.2(i) restricted to LNAs, with the citation followed through:

> **An LNA is derived tame iff its Euler form `h_A(x) = x^T(C_A^{-1} + C_A^{-T})x`
> is positive semidefinite** (Brüstle, Theorem 1.1; every LNA is a tree algebra).

Derived tameness is a derived invariant, so this is a genuine two-valued
separator: **a derived tame LNA is never derived equivalent to a derived wild
one.** It is not subsumed by the Coxeter polynomial — at `n = 10` the polynomial
`T^10 + T^9 + T + 1` is carried by 321 derived tame LNAs *and* by the two derived
wild ones `34504030`, `50505000`, which are exactly `Λ` and `Λ'` of
arXiv:2310.08346's `example:A10double`. Their Euler forms have least eigenvalue
`−0.462` and `−0.183`; every other LNA with that polynomial has a non-negative
one. **So the Euler form separates the `n = 10` cospectral group that the Coxeter
polynomial cannot** — and F-010's pair is separated by definiteness, not by a new
polynomial. (`3033030` at `n = 9` is the boundary case: least eigenvalue `0`, so
non-negative, so derived **tame** despite not being piecewise hereditary.)

And the full strength of the citation — the complete merging criterion for the
derived tame LNAs — is in
[bruestle-derived-tame-tree-algebras.md](bruestle-derived-tame-tree-algebras.md).
