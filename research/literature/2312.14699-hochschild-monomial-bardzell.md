# The Hochschild cohomology ring of monomial algebras

**arXiv:** [2312.14699](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14699) · D. Artenstein,
J. C. Letz, A. Oswald, A. Solotar

Bardzell's minimal bimodule resolution (Bardzell, *The alternating syzygy
behavior of monomial algebras*, J. Algebra 188 (1997)) written out in full,
plus an explicit diagonal map and the cup product it induces.

We went to it for idea 22's surviving candidate: Hochschild cohomology as a
derived invariant that does not care about relation lengths, to separate the
cospectral pair of F-010.

**It closes idea 22.** The paper gives everything needed to compute `HH*(A)` for
an LNA, and what it computes is `k`, in degree 0, for **every** LNA. The
derivation is four lines and is below. This file exists so nobody spends a week
implementing Bardzell before finding that out.

## The combinatorics: ambiguities

Paths compose left-to-right in the paper's convention: `p = α_n … α_1` with
`t(α_i) = s(α_{i+1})`, and `qp` means `p` then `q`. `B` is the (unique) basis of
nonzero paths of the monomial algebra `A = kQ/I`.

> **Definition 2.2.** A path is a **left `n`-ambiguity** if it decomposes as
> `p = u_{-1} u_0 … u_n` with `u_{-1} ∈ Q_0`, `u_0 ∈ Q_1`, `u_i ∈ B`, and for
> every `0 ≤ i ≤ n − 1`: `u_i u_{i+1} ∈ I` and **no proper suffix of
> `u_i u_{i+1}` lies in `I`**. Dually for right `n`-ambiguities, with prefix for
> suffix.

Left and right ambiguities coincide as sets (Bardzell Lemma 3.1), so one says
**`n`-ambiguity** and writes `Γ_n`. This is `AP(n+1)` in Bardzell and `AP_{n+1}`
in Redondo–Román.

The low degrees, which is all we need:

- `Γ_{-1} = Q_0`, the vertices;
- `Γ_0 = Q_1`, the arrows;
- `Γ_1` = the **minimal generating set of paths of `I`** — our relations;
- `Γ_2` = paths that are the union of two minimal relations **overlapping in a
  nonempty subpath** (`u_1` cannot be trivial: `u_0 u_1 ∈ I` with `u_0` an
  arrow forces `u_1 ≠ e`);
- `Γ_n` = chains of `n` relations, each overlapping the next.

So `Γ_*` **is** the overlap combinatorics `overlap.overlapProfile` measures,
promoted to a homological index. Note that an overlap of a *single arrow* — the
almost-separate bound of arXiv:2305.06642 — already produces a 2-ambiguity;
only pairwise **disjoint** relations give `Γ_2 = ∅`.

**Corollary 2.8.** No proper divisor of an `n`-ambiguity is an `n`-ambiguity,
and its decompositions as a left and as a right ambiguity are unique.

**Lemma 3.3** (= Bardzell 3.3). For `n` **even**, an `n`-ambiguity `p` contains
exactly two `(n−1)`-ambiguities, namely `{ ⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾p , p⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾ }`. For `n` odd there
may be many — this alternation is the "alternating syzygy behaviour" of
Bardzell's title, and it is why the differential below has two cases.

## Theorem 3.6 — Bardzell's resolution

> Let `A = kQ/I` be monomial, `E := kQ_0`, `A^e := A ⊗_k A^op`. Then
>
>     Bzl(A)_{n+1} := A ⊗_E kΓ_n ⊗_E A      (n ≥ −1),   0 otherwise
>
> with differential, for `p ∈ Γ_n`,
>
>     d_{n+1}(1 ⊗ p ⊗ 1) =
>        Σ_{q ∈ Sub(p)} suf(q) ⊗ q ⊗ pre(q)                        n odd
>        suf(p⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾) ⊗ p⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾ ⊗ 1  −  1 ⊗ ⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾p ⊗ pre(⁽ⁿ⁻¹⁾p)      n even
>
> is a **minimal** resolution of `A` over `A^e`, with augmentation
> `ε(b ⊗ p ⊗ a) = ba` for `p ∈ Γ_{-1}` and 0 otherwise.

Here `Sub(p) = {q ∈ Γ_{n-1} : q ≤ p}`, and `p = suf(q) q pre(q)` fixes the
position.

## The complex that computes HH*, and its basis (Section 5)

This is the statement to implement.

> By Hom-tensor adjunction,
> `Hom_{A^e}(Bzl_{n+1}(A), A) ≅ Hom_{E^e}(kΓ_n, A) =: kΓ_n‖B`,
> **with basis the pairs `p‖b` where `p ∈ Γ_n`, `b ∈ B`, and
> `s(p) = s(b)`, `t(p) = t(b)`** — that is, `p` and `b` are **parallel paths**.
> (`(p‖b)(q) = b` if `q = p`, else 0.)

Differential, for `p‖b ∈ kΓ_{n−1}‖B` evaluated at `q ∈ Γ_n`:

    (5.1)  n even:   0                         if p ⊀ q
                     cb − ba                   if cp = pa = q
                     cb                        if cp = q, p not a suffix of q
                     −ba                       if pa = q, p not a prefix of q

    (5.2)  n odd:    0                         if p ⊀ q
                     Σ_{i=1}^N c_i b a_i       where c_i p a_i = q, a_i distinct

Cup product (5.3), for `p_1‖b_1 ∈ kΓ_{i}`, `p_2‖b_2 ∈ kΓ_{j}` and
`q` an `(i+j−1)`-ambiguity:

    (p_2‖b_2 ⌣ p_1‖b_1)(q) = Σ_{q = e p_2 c p_1 a}  e b_2 c b_1 a

## Theorem A (= Theorem 6.5) — the ring structure is trivial for us

> Let `A = kQ/I` be a **triangular** monomial algebra (triangular = the quiver
> has no oriented cycles). Then the cup product on `HH*(A)` is **zero in
> positive degrees**.

Proved via **Lemma 6.4**: for irreducible cocycles `x, y` of positive degree,
`x ⌣ y ≠ 0` and `y ⌣ x ≠ 0` together would force an oriented cycle in `Q`; with
graded-commutativity that kills the class. **Lemma 6.3** is the technical core:
an irreducible cocycle over a triangular monomial algebra has all its summands
`p_i‖b_i` of the form `c_i p̃ a_i ‖ c_i b̃ a_i` for one fixed pair `p̃, b̃`.

This settles a conjecture of Bustamante (2006), who had the quadratic string
case; Redondo–Román had the string case.

## The corollary that closes idea 22

LNAs are monomial and triangular, so Theorem 6.5 already says the **ring**
structure of `HH*` carries no information for us. But the graded vector space
collapses too, and the reason is the word **parallel** in the basis of
`kΓ_n‖B`.

On the linearly oriented `A_n` there is **at most one path between any ordered
pair of vertices**. So for `p ∈ Γ_n`, the only path parallel to `p` is `p`
itself, and `p‖b` exists iff `p ∈ B`, i.e. iff `p ≠ 0` in `A`. For `n ≥ 1`,
every `p ∈ Γ_n` has `u_0 u_1 ∈ I` as a subpath, so `p ∈ I` and `p ∉ B`. Hence

    kΓ_n‖B = 0   for all n ≥ 1,      so   HH^m(A) = 0 for all m ≥ 2.

What survives is `0 → kΓ_{-1}‖B → kΓ_0‖B → 0`, i.e. `k^{Q_0} → k^{Q_1}`, which
by (5.1) is the incidence map `e_i ↦ [i = t(α)] − [i = s(α)]` of the line graph:
rank `n − 1`, kernel spanned by `Σ_i e_i‖e_i`. Therefore, **for every LNA**

    HH^0(A) = k,    HH^m(A) = 0 for all m ≥ 1,    HH*(A) = k.

No dependence on `I` at all. Checked by hand on `k(1→2→3)/(α₂α₁)` and
`k(1→2→3→4)/rad²`; consistent with `HH*(kT) = k` for a tree `T`, which is what
every quipu partner is, and consistent with the Cartan determinant of an LNA
being 1.

**So Hochschild cohomology cannot separate any two LNA classes — the cospectral
pair least of all.** R-008 left idea 22 standing on this one leg; the leg is
gone. Anything in the derived class of an LNA — including every mutated quiver
with parallel arrows the search produces — also has `HH* = k`, since `HH*` is a
derived invariant; that is a free correctness check on the search, and nothing
more.

## Caveats and limitations

- **Do not read Theorem A as "monomial algebras have trivial `HH*`".** It is the
  *cup product in positive degrees* that vanishes, for *triangular* algebras.
  Example 5.5 is a string algebra (with a cycle, `I = (βζ, ζγ, αζα, ζαζ)`) whose
  `HH*` is infinite-dimensional, not finitely generated, and has nonzero products
  — and the `HH^i` there are nonzero in every degree. Triangularity, not
  monomiality, is what flattens things.
- **Example 6.6** is a warning against the obvious shortcut: two irreducible
  cocycles over a triangular monomial algebra with `y ⌣ x ≠ 0` **as a cochain**.
  It is a coboundary, so zero in cohomology, but the cochain-level product is
  genuinely nonzero. Any implementation must pass to cohomology before
  concluding.
- **Ambiguities do not always extend.** Example 3.5 exhibits a 2-ambiguity that
  is *not* the truncation of any 3-ambiguity. So `Γ_n` cannot be built by naively
  growing `Γ_{n-1}`; Lemma 3.2 says when extension is possible and it needs
  hypotheses.
- **Quadratic is special.** Example 2.4: when `I` is generated by paths of length
  2, every `u_i` in a left ambiguity is an arrow and every `u_i u_{i+1}` is a
  1-ambiguity. With longer relations `u_i u_{i+1}` can have a proper prefix in
  `I` and none of that holds — the situation in Example 2.5. Results quoted for
  quadratic monomial algebras (e.g. Example 5.4's cup product formula, or
  Redondo–Román) do **not** transfer to LNAs with a relation of three or more
  arrows.
- The diagonal map (Theorem 4.3), which is the paper's actual novelty, we do not
  use: it only matters for the ring structure, and the ring structure is zero
  for us.

## What it does not give us

No derived invariant that distinguishes LNAs — it proves the opposite. No
mutation, no merge. Its value here is entirely negative, and entirely worth
having.

## For LNAs specifically

Drop idea 22. `HH*` is `k` for every LNA, so the `(dim HH^0, dim HH^1, …)`
sequence is constant on our whole problem and separates nothing. Together with
F-019 (the AAG route is empty because no non-hereditary class contains a gentle
algebra) that closes two of the three "cheap independent invariant" routes on
the candidate list. The third, the `q`-Cartan determinant that Bessenrodt–Holm
build combinatorial derived invariants from (math/0504547, **not read** in this
sweep), is worth one minute and no more: the Cartan matrix of an LNA is
unitriangular — `c_{ij} = 1` iff the path `i → j` is nonzero — and stays
unitriangular when entries are weighted `q^{j-i}`, so `det C_A(q) = 1` for every
LNA. What remains is the Coxeter polynomial, which F-010 says fails
exactly at cospectral quipus, and constructions — arXiv:1504.02617,
arXiv:1009.3370 Theorem 2.32 — that produce equivalences rather than obstruct
them.

The one thing to keep from the paper is `Γ_*`: the `n`-ambiguities of an LNA are
the chains of mutually overlapping relations, which is the same index set as the
higher-degree arrows of the dg quiver in
`1504.02617-quivers-for-silting-mutation.md`. Two unrelated papers arriving at
the same combinatorics is a hint about where the real invariant lives, even
though neither of them hands it to us.
