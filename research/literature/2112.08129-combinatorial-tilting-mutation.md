# A combinatorial procedure for tilting mutation

**arXiv:** [2112.08129](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08129) · D. Fosse

The rule the whole codebase implements. Tilting mutation of a path algebra,
performed entirely by rewriting the quiver with relations.

## What it gives us

Given a quiver with admissible relations `(Q, I)` and a vertex `i`, it constructs
a new quiver `m_i(Q)` whose path algebra is isomorphic to
`End_Λ(μ_i^R(Λ))^op` — the right tilting mutation at the indecomposable
projective `P_i`. So derived equivalent algebras can be generated by a purely
combinatorial walk.

Implemented in `quiverMutationAtVertex`.

## The procedure, in seven steps

1. **Compositions through `i`** become arrows: for `β: h → i` and `α: i → j`, add
   `αβ: h → j`.
2. **Arrows out of `i` flip**: `α: i → j` becomes `α*: j → i*`.
3. **Relations out of `i` become arrows**: a minimal relation `r: i ⇢ k` becomes
   `r̄: i* → k`. *On a cycle*, a relation `r: i ⇢ i` gives one arrow
   `α r̄: i* → t(α)` **per arrow `α` out of `i`**.
4. **Arrows into `i` become relations**: each `β: h → i` gives
   `Σ_α α* α β = 0`, summed over the arrows out of `i`.
5. **Relations for new compositions through `i*`**: `r̄ α* = r/α`, where `r/α` is
   the part of `r` beginning with `α`, with `α` removed.
6. **Relations into `i` extend**: a relation ending at `i` gives one ending at
   `t(α)` for each arrow `α` out of `i`.
7. **Relations out of `i*`**: quoted, because the abridged version of this was
   read wrongly once and cost a day (R-007):

   > A linear combination of paths from `i*` to some vertex `l` defines a
   > relation in `m_i(Q)` **if and only if** precomposing it with each arrow
   > `α*: t(α) -> i*` gives a relation in the quiver `Q`. Specifically, if `R`
   > is a subset of the set of relations in `Q` which begin in `i`, and
   > `{ε_r}` is a collection of **(linear combinations of) paths `t(r) -> l`**,
   > then `Σ_r ε_r r̄ = 0` is a relation `i* ⇢ l` in `m_i(Q)` if and only if
   > `Σ_r ε_r (r/α) = 0` is a relation in `Q` for each `α` starting in `i`.

   Two things in that are easy to miss and both matter. The `ε_r` are **paths,
   not scalars**, and `l` is **any** vertex — so step 7 is about every path out
   of `i*`, `r̄` followed by a tail, not only the arrows `r̄` themselves. And it
   is an **iff**, which makes it a kernel: the relations out of `i*` to `l` are
   exactly the combinations that every `α` sends into `I`. That is how
   `procedure.py` computes it.

**Note after step 7.** A relation containing a path of length one says that arrow
equals a combination of paths, so both may be removed. This is the cleanup
`reducePathAlgebra` performs.

## Admissibility — when mutation is allowed

The theorem's own hypotheses are on the algebra:

- no arrow `i → i`;
- `Hom_Λ(P_i*[1], Λ) = 0`.

The paper is explicit that the second **is not equivalent to a condition on the
quiver**. What it gives instead is a theorem naming two cases where mutation is
*impossible*: no arrow out of `i` at all, or **there exists a nonzero path
ending in `i` whose composite with every arrow out of `i` is zero**. With a
single arrow `α` out of `i` that specialises to: a minimal zero relation ending
with `α`.

So the quiver criterion rules mutations *out*; it does not rule them *in*. Two
implementations of it:

- `procedure.isMutable` is the paper's criterion, read exactly, with "nonzero"
  decided over the ideal.
- `mutation.mutationIsPossibleAtVertex` is **stricter**: it rejects as soon as
  *one* arrow out of the vertex kills a nonzero path, where the paper asks that
  *every* one does. The two agree whenever the vertex has a single arrow out,
  which is every vertex of an LNA — they part company only after a mutation has
  made a vertex branch.

The stricter one stays the search's gate. Since neither is exactly
`Hom(P_i*[1], Λ) = 0`, erring towards refusing is the safe direction: it loses
reachability, where the permissive one risks performing a mutation that is not a
derived equivalence. **That risk is real, not theoretical** — R-005 is 38 "rules"
admitted on exactly that mistake. F-015 has the measured difference.

## Caveats

- **Left mutation** is the entirely dual result: the same procedure with every
  arrow reversed. `leftQuiverMutationAtVertex` does dual → right mutation → dual.
- **Step 3's cyclic case is not implemented here** (F-002). The paper's second
  worked example does not reproduce. Harmless for LNAs, where the search stops at
  the first cycle.
- The procedure's relations are **linear combinations of paths**, and steps 4, 5
  and 7 all produce coefficients the set-of-paths model cannot record. That is
  why `procedure.py` exists and why the set-of-paths model is now only the
  storage format — F-015, and F-005, F-006 for what the loss cost.

## Used in tests

`tests/test_mutation_procedure.py` reproduces the acyclic worked example step by
step, and pins the cyclic gap as a strict xfail.
`tests/test_procedure.py` checks the coefficient-carrying implementation against
the set-of-paths one it replaced, and pins step 5's minus sign and step 7's
kernel.
