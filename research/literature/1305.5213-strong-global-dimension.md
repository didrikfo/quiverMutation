# The strong global dimension of piecewise hereditary algebras

**arXiv:** [1305.5213](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.5213) · E. R. Alvares,
P. Le Meur, E. N. Marcos · v3, 16 Mar 2017 · *read 2026-09-19 from the arXiv PDF*

Read for two things it carries rather than proves:

- **Happel–Zacharia**, *A homological characterization of piecewise hereditary
  algebras*, Math. Z. **260** (2008), 177–185 — journal-only, not on arXiv. Its
  theorem is stated and used here, so this file records it from here.
- **Happel–Rickard–Schofield**, *Piecewise hereditary algebras*, Arch. Math. **66**
  (1996), 182–186 — likewise journal-only, likewise restated here.

and then for the paper's own contribution, which measures *how far* a piecewise
hereditary algebra is from hereditary.

Setting: `A` finite dimensional over an **algebraically closed** field.

## The invariant

Let `X` be an indecomposable object of `K^b(proj A)` with minimal projective
resolution `0 → P^r → … → P^s → 0`, `P^r ≠ 0 ≠ P^s`. Its **length** is
`ℓ(X) = s − r`, and

    s.gl.dim A = sup { ℓ(X) : X ∈ ind K^b(proj A) } ∈ N ∪ {∞}.

(The original definition, in Skowroński [27], is this plus 1 — **the two
conventions differ by one**, and the paper uses the Happel–Zacharia one. Check
which a citing paper means.)

There is an equivalent formulation used throughout (from [3, Lem. 5.6]): for a
tilting object `T` in a triangulated `T` and `A = End(T)^op`, set
`ℓ⁺_T(X) = sup{n : Hom(X, T[n]) ≠ 0}`, `ℓ⁻_T(X) = inf{n : Hom(T[n], X) ≠ 0}`; then
`s.gl.dim A = sup{ ℓ⁺_T(X) − ℓ⁻_T(X) : X ∈ ind T }`.

Immediate values:

- `s.gl.dim A = 1` ⟺ `A` is hereditary and not semisimple;
- `s.gl.dim A = 2` ⟺ `A` is quasi-tilted and not hereditary (Happel–Zacharia's
  byproduct).

## Results we use

### Happel–Zacharia — the characterisation

> `A` is **piecewise hereditary** if and only if `s.gl.dim A < ∞`.

This settles [19, Problem 1] (Skowroński's question) in general; the radical-square-
zero case had been done in [19]. It is the second characterisation of piecewise
heredity in this sweep, the other being Chen–Ringel's Corollary 5.4 — and this one
is numerical, so it is the more likely of the two to be implementable.

A reformulation also recorded here, via [27]: **`s.gl.dim A < ∞` ⟺ the repetitive
algebra `Â` is locally support finite** in the sense of [9]. And, combining [6,7]
and [27]: **the algebras with both finite strong global dimension and a *tame*
repetitive algebra are exactly the piecewise hereditary algebras with non-negative
Euler form.** That connects this file to
[`1310.1557-algebras-of-cyclotomic-type.md`](1310.1557-algebras-of-cyclotomic-type.md),
whose §6.1 shows non-negative Euler form ⟹ cyclotomic type.

### Happel–Rickard–Schofield — the tilting-chain characterisation

> `D^b(mod A)` is equivalent to `D^b(mod H)` for a finite dimensional **hereditary**
> `H` **if and only if** there is a sequence of algebras starting at such an `H`,
> ending at `A`, in which each term is the opposite of the endomorphism algebra of
> a tilting module over the preceding term. If the sequence has `ℓ + 2` terms then
> `gl.dim A ≤ ℓ + 2`.

Two things to take from this. First, being **derived equivalent to a hereditary
algebra** — i.e. being in a quipu class — is equivalent to being reachable from a
hereditary algebra by a chain of *tilting-module* steps. That is a positive,
constructive characterisation of exactly the property `annotateHereditaryForms`
tries to establish by search. Second, the bound runs the wrong way for us:
`gl.dim A ≤ ℓ + 2` bounds the global dimension by the chain length, not the chain
length by anything we can compute. The paper's own comment: "in many examples,
`gl.dim A` appears to be small whereas `ℓ` is large."

### Theorem 1 — `s.gl.dim` as the depth in Happel's description

> Let `T ≃ D^b(H₀)` for a hereditary abelian category, `T ∈ T` a tilting object,
> `A = End(T)^op` **not hereditary**. There is a hereditary abelian generating
> subcategory `H ⊆ T` with `T ∈ ⋁_{i=0}^{ℓ} H[i]`, and
> 1. `s.gl.dim A ≤ ℓ + 2` for **any** such pair `(H, ℓ)`;
> 2. **some** such pair achieves `s.gl.dim A = ℓ + 2`.

So `s.gl.dim A − 2` is exactly the minimal number of shifts a tilting object for
`A` has to spread over, minimised across all hereditary hearts.

### Theorem 2 — `s.gl.dim` as tilting-mutation distance to quasi-tilted

> With `T`, `T` as above and `End(T)^op` not hereditary, there are `ℓ ≥ 0` and
> tilting objects `T^{(0)}, …, T^{(ℓ)} = T` in `T` such that `End(T^{(0)})^op` is
> **quasi-tilted** and each `T^{(i)}` is obtained from `T^{(i−1)}` by a single
> **tilting mutation**. For any such sequence `s.gl.dim End(T)^op ≤ ℓ + 2`, and
> there is a sequence with `s.gl.dim End(T^{(i)})^op = 2 + i` for every `i`.

Here a *tilting mutation* is: split `T = T₁ ⊕ T₂` with `Hom(T₂, T₁) = 0`, take a
triangle `T'₂ → M → T₂ → T'₂[1]` with `M → T₂` a minimal right `add T₁`-
approximation, and set `T' = T₁ ⊕ T'₂`. **This is the same operation the repo's
mutation implements**, in the triangulated formulation.

So `s.gl.dim A = 2 + (minimal number of tilting mutations from a quasi-tilted
algebra to A)`. That is a statement about the geometry of the mutation graph
inside one derived class, which is our object of study.

### §3.2 Proposition — one mutation moves `s.gl.dim` by at most one

> `| s.gl.dim End(T)^op − s.gl.dim End(T')^op | ≤ 1` when `T'` is obtained from `T`
> by a tilting mutation.

Read together with Theorem 2 this is a **Lipschitz property of the mutation
graph**: `s.gl.dim` is a height function on the class, changing by at most 1 per
mutation, with minimum 1 (hereditary) or 2 (quasi-tilted). If we ever want a
potential function to guide or bound the mutation search within a class, this is
one, and it is sharp.

### §4 — computing the value

`s.gl.dim End(T)^op` is determined by which Auslander–Reiten components of `T` the
indecomposable summands of `T` start and end in. The two clean cases:

- **§4.2.4 / 5.2**: if `H` arises from a weighted projective line, `T` starts in
  `H₀` (the torsion/finite-length part) and ends in `H₀[ℓ]`, then
  `s.gl.dim End(T)^op = ℓ + 2`.
- **§5.3**: if `T` starts in `H` and ends in `H[ℓ]` with all summands in
  transjective components, then `s.gl.dim End(T)^op = ℓ + 2`.
- **§4.2.2 Lemma**: `T` starting in `H₀` and ending in `H₀[1]` forces
  `s.gl.dim End(T)^op = 3` and forces all relevant summands into a **single tube**.

## Caveats and limitations

- **`s.gl.dim` is NOT a derived invariant.** Only its finiteness is. The §3.2
  Proposition says one tilting mutation can change it, and a tilting mutation
  preserves the derived class — so two derived equivalent algebras can have
  different strong global dimensions (`1` for a hereditary algebra, `2` for a
  quasi-tilted non-hereditary one derived equivalent to it). **Do not use the value
  as a separating invariant.** What is invariant is `s.gl.dim < ∞`, and by
  Theorem 2 the *minimum* of `s.gl.dim` over the derived class, which is 1 for a
  quipu class and 2 otherwise.
- **Algebraically closed field** is assumed from the first line, and Happel's
  classification of hereditary abelian categories is used throughout §4–5.
- The whole paper **assumes `A` is already piecewise hereditary**. It computes
  `s.gl.dim` for such `A`; it gives no way to decide finiteness, which is what we
  would actually want from Happel–Zacharia. Deciding `s.gl.dim A < ∞` directly
  means bounding the lengths of indecomposables in `K^b(proj A)` over an infinite
  family, and nothing here does that.
- Theorem 1 and Theorem 2 are existence statements about *some* hereditary heart /
  *some* mutation sequence achieving the bound. They do not hand you the sequence.
- Happel–Zacharia and Happel–Rickard–Schofield are **quoted here, not proved**.
  Both are journal-only; this file is secondary for those two statements.

## What it does not give us

- No derived invariant we can compute and compare, beyond the boolean
  "piecewise hereditary".
- No merge, and nothing about Nakayama algebras specifically — LNAs appear nowhere.
- No algorithm. The characterisation of `s.gl.dim` is in terms of AR components of
  a derived category we do not construct.

## How it lands on our problem

The usable item is **Theorem 2 plus the §3.2 Proposition**: within one derived
class, `s.gl.dim` is a height function on the mutation graph that changes by at
most 1 per mutation and bottoms out at a quasi-tilted (or hereditary)
representative. For a **quipu class** the bottom is 1, attained at the quipu
itself. That gives a lower bound on the mutation distance from an LNA to its
quipu — `s.gl.dim(LNA) − 1` mutations at least — which is exactly the quantity
`annotateHereditaryForms`' search depth is fighting. If `s.gl.dim` of an LNA can
be computed (it is a string algebra; indecomposables of `K^b(proj)` are homotopy
strings), this turns "search deeper" into a number known in advance.

Happel–Zacharia itself is a characterisation we cannot run, but it is worth
recording as the statement that "not piecewise hereditary" has a purely numerical
meaning — unbounded lengths in `K^b(proj)` — independent of paths, `τ`-orbits or
weighted projective lines.
