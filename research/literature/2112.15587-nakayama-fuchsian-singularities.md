# Nakayama algebras and Fuchsian singularities

**arXiv:** [2112.15587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.15587) · H. Lenzing, H. Meltzer,
S. Ruan · v2, 7 Feb 2022 · *read 2026-09-18 from the arXiv PDF*

The single most useful paper in this sweep. It settles, completely, the derived
equivalence classification of the **radical-power LNAs** `N_n(r) = kA_n / rad^r`,
in three ways at once: it **merges** them into named classes, it **names** the
class (hereditary star, weighted projective line, or Fuchsian singularity), and it
gives a **spectral obstruction** to piecewise heredity that we do not have.

It also restates Happel–Seidel's Table 1 in full and proves it a second way — see
[`happel-seidel-piecewise-hereditary-nakayama.md`](happel-seidel-piecewise-hereditary-nakayama.md),
which is the file for that classification as such.

## Notation, and the translation into ours

`N_n(r)` is `kA_n` modulo every path of `r` arrows. Our class name for it is the
digit string of relation lengths, which has `n - 2` digits:

    N_n(r)  =  "r" * (n - r)  +  "0" * (r - 2)

so `N_9(3) = 3333330`, `N_12(3) = 3333333330`, `N_11(4) = 444444400`,
`N_12(7) = 7777700000`. The paper's second parameter is almost always written
`n - r`, **the number of relations**, and its figures are indexed by `(n - r, r)`.
The standing hypotheses of the classification theorems are `n ≥ 5`, `r ≥ 3`,
`n - r ≥ 2`, over an **algebraically closed** field.

Three symbols for derived types, used throughout:

| symbol | means |
|---|---|
| `[a,b,c]` | `D^b` of the hereditary **star** with three branches of lengths `a,b,c` |
| `(a,b,c)` | `D^b(coh X)` for the weighted projective line `X(a,b,c)` — canonical type |
| `⟨a,b,c]` | the **stable category of vector bundles** `vect^Z-X(a,b,c)`, equivalently `D^b` of the *extended* canonical algebra — "Fuchsian type" |

`[a,b,c]` is the only one of the three that is a quipu (a star is a quipu with one
foot). `(a,b,c)` is piecewise hereditary but of sheaf, not module, type. `⟨a,b,c]`
is **not piecewise hereditary at all** (Proposition 4.3).

## Results we use

### Proposition 4.1 — the (extended) Happel–Seidel symmetry
*This is the merge rule.*

> Let `a, b ≥ 2` and put `n = (a − 1)(b − 1)`. Then
> 1. `D^b(N_n(a)) ≃ D^b(N_n(b))`;
> 2. `D^b(N_{n−1}(a)) ≃ D^b(N_{n−1}(b))`;
> 3. `D^b(N_{n+1}(a)) ≃ D^b(N_{n+1}(b))`.

Part (1) is Happel–Seidel's own symmetry (the paper attributes the proof to
Kussin–Lenzing–Meltzer, *Triangle singularities, ADE-chains, and weighted
projective lines*, Adv. Math. 237, Thm. 6.11): both sides are the stable category
`vect-X(2,a,b)`, which is symmetric in `a` and `b`. Parts (2) and (3) are this
paper's extension, proved by perpendicular category / one-point extension of an
Auslander bundle.

**Directly usable.** For `a = 3, b = 5` we get `n = 8`, so `333330 ≃ 555000`
(6 digits); offset `−1` gives `n = 7`, `33330 ≃ 55000`; offset `+1` gives `n = 9`,
`3333330 ≃ 5555000`. One merge per `(a,b)` and per offset, both members inside a
single `n`. Verified cospectral here for every `a, b ≤ 5` and all three offsets
(a necessary check, not a proof).

Note that (1) needs `n = (a−1)(b−1) ≥ max(a,b) + 1` for both sides to be defined
as stated; the degenerate pairs (`a = b`, or `a = 2`) give nothing.

### Theorem 4.4 — the piecewise hereditary classification (Happel–Seidel)

The complete list, for `n ≥ 5`, `r ≥ 3`. Sheaf type:

- the family `N_{r+4}(r)`, `r ≥ 4`, of type `(2,3,r)`;
- `N_9(3)`, `N_9(6)`, `N_9(7)` of type `(2,3,5)`;
- `N_11(3)`, `N_11(6)` of type `(2,3,7)`;
- `N_10(3)` of type `(2,3,6)`; `N_9(4)` of type `(2,4,4)`; `N_10(4)` of type `(2,4,5)`.

Module type (i.e. **quipu** type — every one of these is a star):

- the family `N_{r+2}(r)`, `r ≥ 3`, of type `[2,3,r−1]`;
- the family `N_{r+3}(r)`, `r ≥ 3`, of type `[2,3,r]`;
- `N_9(3)`, `N_9(5)` of type `[2,3,6]`;
- `N_7(3)` of type `[2,3,4]`; `N_8(3)` of type `[2,3,5]`; `N_8(4)` of type `[2,4,4]`;
  `N_10(5)` of type `[2,3,7]`.

Overlaps (tame cases where sheaf and module type coincide):
`(2,3,4) ≃ N_8(4) ≃ [2,4,4]` (affine `Ẽ7`) and
`(2,3,5) ≃ N_9(3) ≃ N_9(5) ≃ N_9(6) ≃ N_9(7) ≃ [2,3,6]` (affine `Ẽ8`).

Excluded from the statement, and true separately: `N_n(2)` is of type `A_n`, and
`N_n(n−1)` is of type `D_n` for `n ≥ 4`.

**Everything not on this list is not piecewise hereditary**, hence in no quipu
class. That is a *complete* answer where our `piecewiseHereditary` criteria give
only sufficient conditions.

### Lemma 4.2 — heredity descends along the last projective

> If `N_n(r)` is piecewise hereditary then so is `N_{n−1}(r)`.

Because `P_n` is exceptional and `P_n^⊥ ≃ D^b(N_{n−1}(r))`. Note the shape: it
deletes the **last** vertex and *keeps `r` fixed*, so in digit strings it drops one
copy of `r` from the front of the run. This is a different, sharper move than
`removevertex` of arXiv:2310.08346 (which extends the relations meeting the deleted
vertex). Contrapositively: `N_m(r)` not piecewise hereditary ⟹ `N_n(r)` not, for
every `n ≥ m`. That is the "fencing" argument that makes the classification finite.

### Proposition 4.3 — Fuchsian ⟹ not piecewise hereditary

> A Fuchsian singularity category cannot be piecewise hereditary.

Proof by AR components: `vect^Z-X` has all components of type `ZA_∞`, while a
piecewise hereditary category has tubes (sheaf type) or `Z∆` components (module
type). This is what turns Theorem 6.3's list into non-piecewise-heredity
certificates.

### Theorem 6.3 — the Fuchsian classification

For `n ≥ 5`, `r ≥ 3`, `N_n(r)` is of Fuchsian type exactly in these cases:

1. the families `N_{r+5}(r)`, `r ≥ 7`, of type `⟨2,3,r]`; and `N_{r+6}(r)`, `r ≥ 6`,
   of type `⟨2,3,r+1]`;
2. hypersurface cases: `N_r(3)` for `r = 12,13,14`, type `⟨2,3,r−5]`; `N_r(4)` and
   `N_r(5)` for `r = 11,12,13`, type `⟨2,5,r−7]`; `N_13(6)`, type `⟨2,4,7]`;
3. complete-intersection cases: `N_15(3) ≃ N_15(8)`, type `⟨2,3,10]`;
   `N_14(4) ≃ N_14(6)`, type `⟨2,5,7]`; `N_14(5)`, type `⟨2,6,6]`; `N_16(8)`,
   type `⟨2,3,11]`.

**Part (3) is conditional** — see the caveats.

Every algebra in this list is a *further* merge rule (`N_15(3) ≃ N_15(8)`,
`N_14(4) ≃ N_14(6)`, `N_11(4) ≃ N_11(5)`, `N_12(4) ≃ N_12(5)`, `N_13(4) ≃ N_13(5)`)
**and** a non-piecewise-hereditary certificate, by Proposition 4.3.

Cross-check: `N_12(3)`, `N_11(4)`, `N_11(5)`, `N_12(6)`, `N_12(7)` are exactly the
five algebras arXiv:2310.08346's `liste:baseforHS` recovers without algebraic
closedness. They are the leftmost column of the "red wall".

### The periodicity criterion (proof of Theorem 6.3, "the green wall")

This is the one genuinely new **spectral** tool here, and it is stated only inside
a proof:

> Periodicity of the Coxeter transformation only appears for **Dynkin module
> type**, and for **sheaf type**, where the possible Coxeter numbers for weight
> triples are **3, 4 and 6**. For Fuchsian type, [23, Table 2] lists only **22
> weight triples** with a periodic Coxeter transformation.

So: **if `Φ_Λ` is periodic of order `m`, and `Λ` is not of Dynkin type, and
`m ∉ {3,4,6}`, then `Λ` is not piecewise hereditary.** Equivalently: if the Coxeter
polynomial is a product of cyclotomic polynomials `Φ_d` whose `d`'s have an l.c.m.
outside the allowed set, the algebra is certified. We have no criterion of this
shape; it costs one factorisation of a polynomial we already compute.

> **Tried, 2026-09-19, and the two exclusions are the whole criterion.** Applied
> to every LNA at `n = 9` as "periodic and `m ∉ {3,4,6}` certifies", it certifies
> **273 of them**, the very first being `0000000` -- the hereditary line `A_9`
> itself, which is piecewise hereditary by definition and whose period 10 is just
> the Dynkin Coxeter number `n + 1`. So "`Λ` is not of Dynkin type" is not a
> footnote to be waved through; it is doing most of the work, and it is the part
> that is hard for us, since deciding Dynkin module type is close to the question
> the criterion was wanted for. Implementing this means implementing the
> **exclusions** -- Dynkin module type, and the 22 Fuchsian weight triples of
> [23, Table 2] -- and a version without them is worse than nothing, because it
> certifies algebras that are piecewise hereditary. The *characteristic
> polynomial* being a product of cyclotomics is also not the same as the
> transformation being periodic (a repeated cyclotomic factor may carry a Jordan
> block), so test `Φ^m = I` on the matrix, not the factorisation. The six wall
> members tabulated below are unaffected: they are certified by the paper, which
> has checked the exclusions.

The paper's §2.7 gives the wall members for `r = 3..8`. **All six recomputed here
on 2026-09-18 and they agree**, both the polynomial and the order of `Φ`:

| `(n,r)` | `χ_{N_n(r)}` | Coxeter number |
|---|---|---|
| `(16,3)` | `(λ+1)(λ^6 − λ^3 + 1)(λ^9 + 1)` | 18 |
| `(15,4)` | `(λ+1)(λ^8 + λ^4 + 1)(λ^6 + 1)` | 12 |
| `(15,5)` | `(λ+1)(λ^4 + 1)(λ^5 + 1)^2` | 40 |
| `(15,6)` | same as `(15,4)` | 12 |
| `(14,7)` | `(λ+1)(λ^6 − λ^3 + 1)(λ^7 + 1)` | 126 |
| `(17,8)` | `(λ+1)(λ^16 + λ^8 + 1)` | 24 |

`(15,4)` and `(15,6)` sharing a polynomial is not a coincidence — it is the
Happel–Seidel symmetry at `a=4, b=6, n=15`, so they are also derived equivalent.
Every one of these six has a Coxeter number outside `{3,4,6}` and is not of Dynkin
type, so **none of them is piecewise hereditary**, by the criterion above. That is
six certificates our `piecewiseHereditary` does not produce, at lengths we cannot
classify by search.

### Proposition 6.1 — a closed-form Coxeter polynomial

> For `r ≥ 9`, `χ_{N_{r+7}(r)} = (λ+1)(λ^6 − λ^3 + 1)(λ^r + 1)`, and the Coxeter
> number is `lcm(2r, 9)`.

A free regression test for `coxeterTables`, in a range we cannot classify. Compare
Dong–Lin–Ruan (arXiv:2203.15735) for the other half of the `(n,r)` plane.

### Conjecture 5.8 — the Coxeter polynomial, conjecturally complete *here*

> Two Nakayama algebras `N_n(a)` and `N_n(b)` are derived equivalent **if and only
> if** they share the same Coxeter polynomial.

Stated for radical powers only, of the same `n`. The paper says it holds between
Nakayama algebras of module type, of sheaf type, and of triangle type, and Remark
6.4 adds Fuchsian type. **This is the sharpest statement anywhere on our central
question**, and it is a conjecture, restricted to a sub-family where every class is
already named. Do not generalise it to LNAs at large — arXiv:2310.08346's
`remark:Coxeter` exhibits a cospectral pair of general LNAs with one piecewise
hereditary and one not, which would be a counterexample if the conjecture were
stated for all LNAs.

## Lemmas worth knowing

- **Proposition 3.1**: an explicit tilting complex in `D^b(coh X(2,3,r))`, `r ≥ 4`,
  with endomorphism algebra `N_{r+4}(r)`. This is the seed for the whole `(2,3,r)`
  column; if we ever want an explicit derived equivalence rather than the mere
  fact, it starts here.
- **Corollary 3.3** (attributed to Happel–Seidel): `N_{r+3}(r)` is derived
  hereditary of type `[2,3,r]` and `N_{r+2}(r)` of type `[2,3,r−1]`, both for
  `r ≥ 3`, by taking perpendicular categories of the last one and last two summands
  of the shifted canonical tilting complex.
- **Remark 3.4**: `N_{r+5}(r)` is the extended canonical algebra of `C(2,3,r)`
  already for `r ≥ 5`; it is only *Fuchsian* for `r ≥ 7`. At `r = 5` it is
  `N_10(5) ≃ [2,3,7]` (module type) and at `r = 6` it is `N_11(6) ≃ (2,3,7)` (sheaf
  type). The family changes type twice before settling — a warning against reading
  a family off its general member.
- **Theorem 5.5**: tilting objects in `vect^Z-X` for weight types `(2,4,5)`,
  `(2,4,7)`, `(2,5,5)`, `(2,5,6)` with Nakayama endomorphism algebras `N_11(5)`,
  `N_13(6)`, `N_12(5)`, `N_13(5)`. These are the hypersurface sporadics.

## Caveats and limitations

- **The field must be algebraically closed.** Happel's trichotomy is used
  throughout. arXiv:2310.08346 makes a point of recovering part of the list
  without that hypothesis; this paper does not.
- **Theorem 6.3 part (3) is conditional on Conjecture 5.7**, and the *completeness*
  of Theorem 6.3 is conditional on Conjecture 6.2 (F2), which is only verified
  experimentally for `3 ≤ r ≤ 50`, `2 ≤ n − r ≤ 50`. Theorem 4.4 (piecewise
  heredity) is **not** conditional — it is proved outright. So: use Theorem 4.4
  freely; treat "Fuchsian type `⟨a,b,c]`" as a name, and the sporadic list as
  provisional.
- **"Fuchsian type" is a positive name for a negative fact.** For us its only
  operational content is Proposition 4.3: not piecewise hereditary. Do not read
  `⟨2,3,7]` as a quipu-like invariant that could merge classes; it merges only the
  pairs the paper explicitly lists.
- **The whole paper is about radical powers.** `rad^r` is one digit repeated. Every
  result here has hypotheses that a general LNA — different relation lengths,
  gaps — fails outright. There is no statement about `3033030`.
- The Happel–Seidel symmetry (Prop. 4.1) is not a *local* move on the digit string:
  `33333000 ≃ 55500000` changes every digit. It cannot be an `lnaMoves` rule, and
  it is not obviously a bounded mutation sequence either.
- Figure 1's "dark green wall" of Coxeter numbers is read off a figure in the PDF,
  which extracts badly. Recompute rather than transcribe.

## What it does not give us

- Nothing about relations of unequal length, which is almost every LNA.
- No invariant finer than the Coxeter polynomial; Conjecture 5.8 goes the other
  way, and is conditional and restricted.
- No explicit derived functor for the Happel–Seidel symmetry in Nakayama terms
  — it is a statement about two tilting objects in a third category. Ueda
  (arXiv:2302.02880) has explicit tilting complexes for the related family; this
  paper does not.
- The periodicity criterion is stated only for what *can* be periodic, not a
  decision procedure: a non-periodic `Φ` says nothing.

## How it lands on our problem

Two concrete jobs:

1. **Merge.** Proposition 4.1 gives, at each `n`, a family of pairs
   `("a"*(n−a) + "0"*(a−2), "b"*(n−b) + "0"*(b−2))` with `n ∈ {(a−1)(b−1) − 1,
   (a−1)(b−1), (a−1)(b−1)+1}` that must be in one class. At `n = 9` this is
   `3333330 ≃ 5555000`; at `n = 8`, `333330 ≃ 555000`. Small `n` is exactly where
   we can check it against a finished classification, and it is the first merge
   rule we have from the literature that is not a local move.
2. **Certify.** Theorem 4.4 is a *complete* answer for the digit strings of the
   form `r^(n−r) 0^(r−2)`. Anything off that list is in no quipu class, with no
   search and no criterion of ours needing to fire. That is an independent check on
   `piecewiseHereditary`: our criteria must never certify an algebra Theorem 4.4
   lists, and Theorem 4.4 should certify many our criteria miss.
