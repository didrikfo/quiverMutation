# Silting mutation in triangulated categories

**arXiv:** [1009.3370](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.3370) · T. Aihara, O. Iyama ·
J. London Math. Soc. 85 (2012) 633–668

The paper that says why our search's gate exists, and what it costs us.

Tilting mutation is **not always possible**: some summands of a tilting object
cannot be replaced. Aihara–Iyama's fix is to enlarge tilting to **silting**,
where mutation is always possible, and then ask separately when a silting
mutation happens to be tilting. Two results matter to us: an **iff** criterion
for that (Theorem 2.32), where arXiv:2112.08129 gives only a necessary
condition; and a **transitivity** theorem (Theorem 3.1) that would make the
search complete for piecewise hereditary LNAs — but over the silting graph, not
the tilting one.

## Definitions, exactly

For `T` triangulated, `M ⊆ T` a full additive subcategory closed under summands:

- `M` is **silting** if `Hom_T(M, M[i]) = 0` for all `i > 0` and `thick M = T`;
- `M` is **tilting** if `Hom_T(M, M[i]) = 0` for all `i ≠ 0` and `thick M = T`.

So tilting = silting + vanishing in *negative* degrees. For `T = K^b(proj A)`,
`A` itself is tilting (Example 2.2(a)), and by Keller (Proposition 2.3) a tilting
object `M` gives `T ≃ K^b(proj End_T(M))` — that is the derived equivalence. A
merely-silting `M` does **not**.

**Existence** (Example 2.5(a)): `D^b(mod A)` contains a silting subcategory iff
`gl.dim A < ∞`. LNAs qualify.

## The results we use

### Theorem 2.31 — mutation is always possible

> Any mutation of a silting subcategory is again silting.

**Definition 2.30**, in the form we need. Let `M` be silting, `D ⊆ M` a
subcategory. For `M ∈ M` take a **left `D`-approximation** `f : M → D` and
complete to a triangle `M →f D →g N_M → M[1]`. Then
`μ⁺(M; D) := add(D ∪ {N_M})` is the **left mutation**. Dually `μ⁻(M; D)` uses a
right `D`-approximation `g : D → M` and the triangle `N_M → D →g M → N_M[1]`.

`μ±_X(M) := μ±(M; M_X)` where `ind M_X = ind M \ ind X`; the mutation is
**irreducible** when `# ind X = 1` — one indecomposable summand replaced. That
is our vertex mutation.

Taking `M = add A` and `X = P_i` in `T = K^b(proj A)`: the right mutation
`μ⁻_{P_i}(A)` is the two-term complex with `P_i` replaced by the cone of a
minimal right `add(⊕_{j≠i} P_j)`-approximation `D_i → P_i`. **This always
exists**, for every `i`, with no admissibility hypothesis at all.

### Theorem 2.32 — when a silting mutation of a tilting object is tilting

This is the one to implement.

> Let `M` be a **tilting** subcategory of `T`.
> **(a)** For a covariantly finite `D ⊆ M`, `μ⁺(M; D)` is tilting **⟺** every
> `M ∈ M` has a left `D`-approximation `f` such that `Hom_T(D, f)` is
> **injective**.
> **(b)** For a contravariantly finite `D ⊆ M`, `μ⁻(M; D)` is tilting **⟺**
> every `M ∈ M` has a right `D`-approximation `g` such that `Hom_T(g, D)` is
> **injective**.

Read (b) at `M = add A`, `D = add(⊕_{j≠i} P_j)`, i.e. right mutation at vertex
`i`. For `M ∈ D` the approximation is the identity and the condition is vacuous,
so **the whole condition sits at `M = P_i`**:

> `μ⁻_{P_i}(A)` is a **tilting** complex — hence `End(μ⁻_{P_i}(A))` is derived
> equivalent to `A` — **if and only if** the map
>
>     Hom(g, D) :  Hom_A(P_i, D)  →  Hom_A(D_i, D),   φ ↦ φ ∘ g
>
> is injective, where `g : D_i → P_i` is a minimal right
> `add(⊕_{j≠i} P_j)`-approximation and `D = ⊕_{j≠i} P_j`.

Everything in that is a finite linear-algebra computation over path spaces:
`Hom_A(P_a, P_b) = e_a A e_b` (left/right convention aside), which for an LNA is
0 or 1 dimensional and is exactly "is the path `a → b` nonzero in `A`".

### Theorem 2.35 and Definition 2.41 — the silting quiver is a Hasse quiver

**Theorem 2.11** puts a partial order on `silt T`: `M ≥ N` iff
`Hom_T(M, N[i]) = 0` for all `i > 0`.

> **Theorem 2.35.** For `M, N ∈ silt T` (under condition (F), satisfied whenever
> `T` is `k`-linear Hom-finite with a silting object), TFAE:
> (a) `N` is an irreducible left mutation of `M`;
> (b) `M` is an irreducible right mutation of `N`;
> (c) `M > N` and there is no `L ∈ silt T` with `M > L > N`.

So the **silting quiver** (Definition 2.41: arrow `M → N` when `N` is an
irreducible left mutation of `M`) is the Hasse quiver of a partial order. Two
consequences we care about: irreducible mutations are exactly the covering
relations, so there are no "shortcuts" to discover; and left and right mutation
are strict inverses (Proposition 2.33), so the search graph is symmetric.

**Proposition 2.36** is the engine of every transitivity proof: if `M > N`, there
is an irreducible *left* mutation `L` of `M` with `M > L ≥ N`. Descent is always
available.

### Theorem 3.1 — transitivity for piecewise hereditary algebras

> Let `T = K^b(proj A)` where `A` is either a **hereditary** algebra or a
> **canonical** algebra. Then the action of iterated **irreducible silting
> mutation** on `silt T` is transitive.

`silt T` depends only on `T` up to triangle equivalence, so this transfers along
derived equivalence: **every piecewise hereditary algebra has property (T)**
(the section is titled "Transitivity for piecewise hereditary algebras"). By
Happel's trichotomy the two cases — hereditary and canonical — cover every
hereditary abelian category with a tilting object.

Proof route, worth knowing because it is constructive: full exceptional
sequences act transitively under the braid group `B_n × Z^n` (Theorem 3.4, due
to Crawley-Boevey / Ringel / Kussin–Meltzer); any full exceptional sequence
gives silting objects after shifting (Proposition 3.5); and two silting objects
differing only by shifts of the members of one exceptional sequence are
connected by irreducible mutations (Lemma 3.6, Proposition 3.7).

### Corollary 2.43 and Theorem 2.44 — local transitivity

> **Theorem 2.44.** If `D` is *almost complete silting* (contained in a silting
> `M` with `#(ind M \ ind D) = 1`) and `thick D` is functorially finite, then
> `{M ∈ silt T | D ⊆ M}` is transitive under iterated irreducible mutation.

The silting analogue of "an almost complete tilting module has exactly two
complements" — except here the fibre is a whole `Z`-chain, not two points
(`μ±_{M[i]}(M[i]) = M[i ± 1]`, Corollary 2.43).

## Caveats and limitations

- **Silting transitivity is not tilting transitivity.** Theorem 3.1 says the
  *silting* quiver is connected. The tilting objects sit inside `silt T` as a
  subset, and a path between two tilting objects will in general pass through
  silting objects that are **not** tilting. A search that refuses every
  non-tilting step — which is what a gate on `isMutable` does — can therefore
  be **incomplete even for a piecewise hereditary algebra**. This is the most
  actionable thing in the paper for us, and it is the opposite of what one
  would guess from "mutation is always possible".
- The paper's own table (p. 2) is blunt about it: tilting mutation is *not*
  always possible and *not* transitive for hereditary algebras. The standing
  counterexample is `DA`: the tilting `A`-module `DA` usually cannot be reached
  from `A` by iterated tilting mutation, even for `A` hereditary.
- **`End` of a silting object is not derived equivalent to `A`.** Only tilting
  gives Proposition 2.3's equivalence. Recording an intermediate silting object
  as a class member would be exactly the R-005 error again, at a different door.
- Property (T) is **open in general** (Question 1.1/2.42); it fails for some
  symmetric algebras [AGI]. Nothing here tells us whether it holds for a
  *non*-piecewise-hereditary LNA — and by arXiv:2310.08346 those exist.
- Approximations must be **minimal** for `N_M` to be indecomposable and for
  mutation to be irreducible (see the proof of Theorem 2.35(i)). A non-minimal
  approximation still gives a silting object, just not an irreducible mutation.
- Theorem 2.32 is about the subcategory `D`, i.e. about mutating a *set* of
  summands. Our vertex mutation is the `#ind X = 1` case; the theorem is
  strictly more general and would license mutating several vertices at once.

## What it does not give us

No quiver-level recipe — the mutation is defined by approximations and
triangles, not by rewriting arrows and relations. That is Oppermann's paper
(arXiv:1504.02617) and ours (arXiv:2112.08129). No invariant, and no statement
about when two *algebras* (rather than two silting objects over one algebra) are
derived equivalent.

## For LNAs specifically

Two concrete jobs.

**1. Replace the gate with an iff.** `procedure.isMutable` implements
arXiv:2112.08129's quiver criterion, which the paper there says plainly rules
mutation *out* and never *in* — the reason R-005 happened, and the reason F-016
had to Coxeter-check every newly allowed mutation. Theorem 2.32(b) is a genuine
**if and only if** for exactly the same question, and its hypothesis is
computable from `e_i A e_j` alone. Writing that test would let the search certify
its own steps instead of checking them after the fact.

**2. Know what the search cannot reach.** Every LNA in a quipu class is
iterated tilted of tree type, hence piecewise hereditary, so Theorem 3.1 applies
and the *silting* quiver over it is connected. It follows that a class missed by
our search is missed because the connecting path leaves the tilting locus, not
because the path is long. A search over silting mutation (always possible,
Theorem 2.31) that records only the nodes passing Theorem 2.32 would be
complete for these algebras — at the price of carrying complexes, not algebras,
through the intermediate steps. That is a design decision the repo has not yet
had to face; this is the paper that forces it.
