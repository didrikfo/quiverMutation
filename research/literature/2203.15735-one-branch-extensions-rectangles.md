# Derived equivalences between one-branch extension algebras of "rectangles"

**arXiv:** [2203.15735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15735) · Q. Dong, Y. Lin,
S. Ruan · 29 Mar 2022 · *read 2026-09-19 from the arXiv PDF*

**`N(n, r)` here is the LINEAR Nakayama algebra.** §4.1 defines it outright: the
path algebra of the equioriented `A_n` quiver `1 ← 2 ← … ← n` modulo all
relations `x^r = 0`, for `2 ≤ r ≤ n - 1`. That is `kA_n / rad^r` — our
`str(r)*(n-r) + "0"*(r-2)` after renumbering `i ↦ n+1-i`, which is an isomorphism
of algebras, not a duality. So everything in this paper lands directly on us.

The paper takes Ladkani's rectangle `A(u) = kA_2 ⊗_k kA_u` (which is
`N(2u, 3)` by Corollary 1.2 of arXiv:0911.5137), attaches a branch of `v`
vertices to it in four different ways, and shows all four are derived equivalent
— to each other and to `N(2u+v, u+v+1)`. Two things fall out that we want: closed
formulas for the Coxeter polynomial of **half** of all radical-power LNAs, and a
merge `N(2r-1, r) ≃ N(2r-1, r+1)`.

## The results we use

### Proposition 4.5 — the merge

> For `r ≥ 2`, `D^b(N(2r-1, r)) ≃ D^b(N(2r-1, r+1))`.

In digit strings: for every `r`,

    str(r)*(r-1) + "0"*(r-2)   ≃   str(r+1)*(r-2) + "0"*(r-1)      on n = 2r-1

`r = 3`: `330 ≃ 400`. `r = 4`: `44400 ≃ 55000`. `r = 5`: `5555000 ≃ 6660000`.
`r = 6`: `666660000 ≃ 777700000`. Coxeter polynomials agree on both sides for
`r = 2 … 8`, checked with `invariants.coxeterKey`.

The proof is two applications of Ladkani's `A_Y` construction (Corollary 4.6 of
arXiv:math/0610685, quoted here as Proposition 2.1) to **one** poset `X`: the
closed subset `Y = {1,…,r-1}` gives `A_Y ≅ N(2r-1, r)`, and Proposition 4.2 with
`u = r-1, v = 1` identifies `I(X)` with something derived equivalent to
`N(2r-1, r+1)`. That is the whole method of the paper and it is worth copying —
see the "how it lands" section below.

**Is this already covered?** Below `n = 11`, yes; from `n = 11` up, no — and that
is where it earns its place.

- **Against Ueda's Corollary 1.3** (`2302.02880-ueda-…`), which merges
  `N(n, ℓ+1) ≃ N(n, ℓ)` for `n = p(p+1)q + p(p-1)ρ`, `ℓ = (p+1)q + pρ`: writing
  `n = p(ℓ - ρ)`, the diagonal `n = 2ℓ - 1` forces `p(ℓ - ρ) = 2ℓ - 1`. For
  `p = 2` this gives `ρ = ½` (Ueda's case (b)), hence `ℓ = 3q + 1`; for `p ≥ 3`
  there is no solution. So **Ueda's corollary meets Proposition 4.5 exactly at
  `r ≡ 1 (mod 3)`** — `r = 4, 7, 10, …`. The cases `r = 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, …` are
  outside it.
- **Against the repo's move table**: `lnaMoves.closureUnderMoves` (default rules)
  joins the two sides only at `r = 2` and `r = 4`. At `r = 5` the orbit of
  `5555000` is a **single element** — no rule in the table matches it — and at
  `r = 6, 7` both orbits are singletons. Neither side is almost separate for
  `r ≥ 4`, so the quipu theorem cannot name them directly either.
- **Against the repo's classification**, which is more than the moves: at `n ≤ 9`
  it already knows every instance. `classify.py 5` puts `330` and `400` in
  `P^(2)_(1,1)`; `classify.py 9` puts `5555000` **and** `6660000` in
  `P^(5)_(1,2)`. The reason is a coincidence that runs out: for `r ≤ 5`, both
  sides also share a Coxeter polynomial with the **single-relation** LNA
  `A_{2r-1,(1)}^{(2r-3)}`, which *is* almost separate, with quipu
  `P^(2r-5)_(1,2)` — so the search has a hereditary quiver to reach. Checked with
  `coxeterKey`: that third algebra matches for `r = 3, 4, 5` and **stops matching
  at `r = 6`** and every `r` up to 10.

So Proposition 4.5 is old news for `r ≤ 5` (`n ≤ 9`) and **new from `r = 6`
(`n = 11`) on**: there the move table does nothing to either side, neither side
is almost separate, the single-relation partner no longer has the right Coxeter
polynomial, and `r ≢ 1 (mod 3)` puts it outside Ueda.

`666660000 ≃ 777700000` at `n = 11` is the first instance we have no other route
to, and it is better than that: **no quipu of order 11 has its Coxeter
polynomial** (checked over all 64 of them), so the class is in *no* quipu class —
this is a proved merge of two LNAs in a **non-hereditary** class, which is
exactly the gap the open problem names.

### Proposition 4.4 — the Coxeter polynomial of `N(n,r)` for `2r ≥ n+2`

Transcribed and **verified against `invariants.coxeterKey` for every `(n,r)` with
`4 ≤ n ≤ 17` and `2r ≥ n+2`: zero mismatches.** Write `s = (-1)^{n-r}`.

**(1) `2r = n+2`:**

    n-r ≢ 1 (mod 3):   χ = (λ+1)(λ^{3n-3r+6} - s) / [ (λ^3+1)(λ^{n-r+2} - s) ]
    n-r ≡ 1 (mod 3):   χ = (λ+1)(λ^{n-r+2} - s)^2 / (λ^3+1)

**(2) `2r ≥ n+3`:**

    n-r ≡ 0 (mod 3):   χ = (λ+1)( λ^{n+2} + s·Σ_{j=0}^{2r-n-2} λ^{n-r+2+j} + 1 ) / (λ^3+1)
    n-r ≡ 1 (mod 3):   χ = (λ+1)( λ^{n-r+2} - s )( λ^r - s ) / (λ^3+1)
    n-r ≡ 2 (mod 3):   χ = (λ+1)( λ^{n+2} - s·Σ_{j=2}^{2r-n-2} λ^{n-r+1+j} + 1 ) / (λ^3+1)

Every one of these is a rational expression that cancels to a polynomial; the
`(λ^3+1)` in the denominator is the trace of the weight type `(2,3,u+v+1)`
weighted projective line the proof goes through.

Two uses. First, **`χ_{N(n,r)}` in closed form for `2r ≥ n+2`** means the Coxeter
polynomial of half the radical-power family without any matrix work, at any `n`.
Second, the agreement over `4 ≤ n ≤ 17` is independent numerical confirmation of
Theorem 1.1 and Propositions 4.1/4.2, which we otherwise take on trust.

### Theorem 1.1 (= Propositions 4.1 + 4.2) — what the family is

> For `u, v ≥ 1`, the incidence algebras of the four bound quivers of Figure 1 —
> the `2 × u` rectangle with a branch of `v` vertices attached at a corner, in
> the four ways one can attach it — are all derived equivalent, and all derived
> equivalent to `N(2u + v, u + v + 1)`.

Set `r = u+v+1`, `n = 2u+v`, so `u = n-r+1` and `v = 2r-n-2`; the condition
`v ≥ 1` is exactly `2r ≥ n+3` and `v = 0` (the plain rectangle) is `2r = n+2`.
That is why the paper reaches precisely the half `2r ≥ n+2`.

**So every radical-power LNA with `2r ≥ n+2` has, as a normal form, a rectangle
with one branch** — a tree-shaped quiver with commutativity relations. That is
the analogue for this range of Ueda's `L(s,t,u)` grid normal form, and it is a
*smaller* class of shapes (two rows only).

### Section 5 — the tilting complexes

§5 realises each of the four one-branch extensions explicitly as
`End` of a tilting complex over `N(2u+v, u+v+1)`, built from two-term complexes
of projectives over the Nakayama algebra. If we ever want a *mutation sequence*
rather than an abstract equivalence for Proposition 4.5, this is where to start:
the complexes are written down, and the repo's procedure works with exactly this
kind of two-term data.

## Lemmas worth knowing

- **Proposition 2.1** is Ladkani's Corollary 4.6 restated with the multiplication
  table of `A_Y` written out — the same formulas as Proposition 4.7 of
  arXiv:math/0610685. If you want the construction in one page, read it here.
- The paper records `A(u) ≃ N(2u, 3)` as Ladkani's, i.e. Corollary 1.2 of
  arXiv:0911.5137 with `m = 2`. Consistent with our reading of that corollary.
- §2.2's conventions: **left** modules, `c_{ij} = dim Hom(P_i, P_j)`, Euler form
  `⟨x,y⟩ = x C^{-t} y^t` on row vectors. Different from Ueda's; check before
  transcribing any matrix.

## Caveats and limitations

- **Radical powers only**, again. Nothing here touches an LNA with two different
  relation lengths.
- **Proposition 4.4 covers only `2r ≥ n+2`**, i.e. relations longer than half the
  line. That is "a half of Nakayama algebras" in the paper's counting but it is
  the *sparse* half for us: at `n = 10` it is `r = 6,7,8,9`, four algebras out of
  4862.
- **`k` is algebraically closed** throughout (weighted projective lines are used
  in the proof of Proposition 4.2). Ueda's overlapping results do not need that.
- **Theorem 1.1's four algebras are not LNAs.** They are incidence algebras of
  posets — commutativity relations, branched quivers. Only `N(2u+v, u+v+1)` is
  one of ours. So the theorem gives one LNA per `(u,v)`, not a merge among LNAs;
  the merge is Proposition 4.5, which comes from choosing two closed subsets.
- **Proposition 4.5 is a single diagonal.** It relates `(n, r)` and `(n, r+1)`
  only when `n = 2r-1`. It says nothing off that line, and gives no
  non-equivalence anywhere.
- The paper gives no invariant and no obstruction; §4.3's Corollary 4.3 is just
  "derived equivalent implies equal Coxeter polynomial" applied to Theorem 1.1.

## What it does not give us

- No local move on digit strings.
- No contact with quipus, piecewise heredity, or the almost-separate case.
- No formula for `χ_{N(n,r)}` when `2r < n+2`, which is where nearly all of our
  algebras live.

## How it lands on our problem

1. **Implement Proposition 4.5 as a merge rule**: for every `r ≥ 2`, join
   `rad^r` and `rad^{r+1}` on `n = 2r-1` vertices. For `r ≤ 5` it agrees with the
   finished classifications; from `r = 6` (`n = 11`) up it is a merge nothing
   else in the repo can make, and it is free.
2. **Copy the method, not just the statement.** The proof is: find one poset `X`,
   take two different closed subsets `Y, Z`, and read off `A_Y` and `A_Z` from
   Proposition 4.7 of arXiv:math/0610685. Whenever both come out as lines with
   zero relations, that is an LNA merge with a proof. This is a *search we could
   run*: enumerate small posets, enumerate their down-sets, compute `A_Y`, keep
   the ones that are lines. It would produce merges of exactly the sort the open
   problem is missing, and unlike a mutation search it is bounded by the number
   of posets rather than by depth.
3. **Use Proposition 4.4 as a test oracle** for any future closed-form Coxeter
   polynomial work on the radical-power family — it is now checked against the
   repo to `n = 17`.
