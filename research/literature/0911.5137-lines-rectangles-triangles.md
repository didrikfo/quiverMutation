# On derived equivalences of lines, rectangles and triangles

**arXiv:** [0911.5137](https://arxiv.org/abs/0911.5137) · S. Ladkani ·
J. London Math. Soc. **87** (2013) 157–176 · *read 2026-09-19 from the arXiv PDF*

The source of the line-to-rectangle equivalence that arXiv:2112.08129 recreates.
A machine for building tilting complexes by tensoring: it takes a tilting complex
`U = U_1 ⊕ … ⊕ U_n` over one algebra `B` and *n* different tilting complexes over
another algebra `A`, and glues them into one tilting complex over `A ⊗_k B` whose
endomorphism ring is a matrix ring that is a **componentwise** — not a global —
tensor product. Applied with `B = kA_n`, it says that a whole ladder of iterated
tilts of `Λ` assembles into an algebra derived equivalent to `Λ ⊗_k kA_n`.

For us the payload is Corollary 1.2, which is a **merge between two radical-power
LNAs outside the almost-separate case**, and the reason it is true.

## Notation, translated

`A(n, m+1)` is `kA_n` modulo the ideal generated by all paths of **`m+1` arrows**
— the paper's "length" is the number of arrows, as ours is. So

    A(n, L)  =  kA_n / rad^L  =  our digit string  str(L) * (n - L) + "0" * (L - 2)

(the digit string has `n - 2` places, and a relation of `L` arrows fits starting
at vertices `1 … n - L`). `kA_m ⊗_k kA_n` is the **rectangle**: the `m × n` grid
quiver with full commutativity relations, equivalently the incidence algebra of
the product poset `A_m × A_n`. `T_n(Λ) = Λ ⊗_k kA_n` is upper-triangular `n × n`
matrices over `Λ`.

## The results we use

### Corollary 1.2 — the line/rectangle equivalence and the `m ↔ n` merge

> Let `k` be a commutative ring and `m, n ≥ 1`. Then `A(m·n, m+1)` and
> `kA_m ⊗_k kA_n` are derived equivalent. In particular `A(m·n, m+1)` and
> `A(m·n, n+1)` are derived equivalent.

No hypothesis on `k` beyond commutative — the paper stresses the equivalence is
"universal", depending only on the combinatorics.

In digit strings the second half reads: for every factorisation `N = m·n`,

    str(m+1) * (N-m-1) + "0"*(m-1)   ≃   str(n+1) * (N-n-1) + "0"*(n-1)

Checked against `invariants.coxeterKey` for every `(m,n)` with `3 ≤ mn ≤ 20` —
27 pairs, zero mismatches. The smallest non-trivial instance is `N = 6`:
`3330 ≃ 4400`.

**It lands outside the quipu theorem.** At `N = 10`, `(m,n) = (2,5)` gives
`33333330 ≃ 66660000`. Neither side has almost separate relations — in
`33333330` the relations `1→4` and `2→5` share two arrows — and the class they
sit in (`22334400` in `A_10_mutation_classes.csv`) has an **empty hereditary
form**, i.e. it is one of the classes that is not a quipu class. So this is
exactly the kind of merge the open problem asks for: a proof, not a search, for a
pair the quipu theorem cannot reach. The repo's search had already joined that
pair; Corollary 1.2 is the reason.

**Relation to Ueda.** Ueda's Corollary 1.3 (`2302.02880-ueda-…`) merges *adjacent*
radical powers at fixed `n`; this merges *conjugate* radical powers at fixed
`n = mn`. They are different families and neither contains the other. Ueda's
Theorem 1.1 with `u = 0` recovers this corollary.

### Corollary 1.3 + Corollary 1.1 — why it is true

`A(mn, m+1)` **is the `(n-1)-replicated algebra of `kA_m`**. That is the content
of the proof: Corollary 1.1 says that for tilting modules `Q_i` over
`Λ_1 = Λ`, `Λ_{i+1} = End_{Λ_i}(Q_i)`, the "ladder" matrix ring

    [ Λ_1                       ]
    [ Q_1  Λ_2                  ]
    [      Q_2  Λ_3             ]
    [               ⋱      Λ_n  ]

is derived equivalent to `T_n(Λ)`; Corollary 1.3 specialises to `Λ` **Gorenstein**
and `Q_i = DΛ` throughout, giving the `(n-1)`-replicated algebra
`[Λ, DΛ; 0, Λ; …]`. For `Λ = kA_m` that replicated algebra *is* the line
`A(mn, m+1)` — §3.2 writes down the isomorphism on bases explicitly
(`e^{(s)}_{ij} ↦ ε_{(s-1)m+i,(s-1)m+j}`, `φ^{(s)}_{ji} ↦ ε_{(s-1)m+j, sm+i}`).

So: **a radical-power line is `m` copies of `kA_m` stacked with `DΛ` between
them, and that is a rectangle in disguise.** Worth remembering because it says
*where* the hidden tensor structure of an LNA lives.

### Corollary 1.4 — replicated algebras inherit merges

> The `n`-replicated algebras of two derived equivalent finite-dimensional
> **Gorenstein** algebras are derived equivalent.

### Corollary 1.9 — the fractional Calabi–Yau dimension

> The line `A(n·m, m+1)` is `((m-1)/(m+1) + (n-1)/(n+1))`-CY.

Being `d/e`-CY (`ν^e ≃ [d]` on `D^b`) is a **derived invariant**, so this is a
separator in principle. Remark 1.14 says that in Corollaries 1.11/1.12 the
fractions are best possible — common factors cannot be cancelled — and the way
he checks that is worth copying: look at the orders of products of pairs of roots
of the two Coxeter polynomials. Note that `ν^e ≃ [d]` forces `Φ^e = (-1)^d I`, so
**a fractionally CY algebra has periodic Coxeter transformation** — which is the
hypothesis of Theorem 3.4 in `math/0611201-coxeter-periodicity-euler-form.md`.

### Theorem B / Theorem C — the general shape

Theorem B: for tilting complexes `T_1, …, T_n ∈ D(Λ)` with
`Hom(T_i, T_{i+1}[r]) = 0` for `r ≠ 0` (consecutive pairs only), the tridiagonal
matrix ring with `End T_i` on the diagonal and `Hom(T_i, T_{i+1})` on the
subdiagonal is derived equivalent to `T_n(Λ)`.

Theorem C: with the stronger hypothesis `Hom(T_i, T_j[r]) = 0` for all `i < j`
and `r ≠ 0`, the *full* lower-triangular matrix ring of `Hom(T_i, T_j)` is derived
equivalent to `T_n(Λ)`.

These are the two ways to spend Theorem A with `B = ZA_n`: Theorem B uses the
simples `S_i[i-1]` as the `U_i`, Theorem C uses the projectives `P_{n+1-i}`
(Proposition 3.1).

## Lemmas worth knowing

- **Proposition 3.1.** Over `RA_n`, three tilting complexes: `⊕P_i` and `⊕I_i`
  both have endomorphism ring `RA_n`, and `S_1 ⊕ S_2[1] ⊕ … ⊕ S_n[n-1]` is
  tilting with `Hom(S_i[i-1], S_j[j-1]) = R` exactly when `j - i ∈ {0,1}`. The
  third is the one that produces lines out of ladders, and it is the reason a
  *line* rather than a rectangle comes out.
- **Corollary 1.7.** If `F` is an auto-equivalence of `per Λ` and
  `e_1 < … < e_n` are integers with `H^r(F^dΛ) = 0` for `r ≠ 0` and
  `H^0(F^{-d}Λ) = 0` for every positive difference `d`, then
  `End(F^{e_1}Λ ⊕ … ⊕ F^{e_n}Λ)` is derived equivalent to `T_n(Λ)`. With
  `F = ν^-[1]` this is Corollary 1.10 and gives the Auslander-algebra results.
- **Corollary 1.13.** The Auslander algebra of the linearly oriented `A_{2n}` is
  derived equivalent to `kA_{2n+1} ⊗_k kA_n`. (The same statement is reproved in
  arXiv:1801.05154 via interval posets.)

## Caveats and limitations

- **Radical powers only.** Every LNA statement here is about `kA_n / rad^L`.
  Nothing in the paper touches an LNA with two different relation lengths, which
  is the case the open problem is about. It merges within a one-parameter family.
- **Corollary 1.2 needs `N` to factor.** For `N` prime the only factorisations
  are `1 × N` and `N × 1`, which give `A(N,2) ≃ kA_N` (already known: length-2
  relations do not matter) and nothing else. So the merge is sparse: at `N = 10`
  it relates only `L = 3` and `L = 6`, and says nothing about `L = 4, 5, 7, 8, 9`.
- **The `m ↔ n` merge is a statement about two algebras, not a local move.** Like
  Happel–Seidel's and Ueda's, it changes every digit at once; it cannot be a
  `(width, before, after, sequence)` rule.
- **Corollary 1.3 requires `Λ` Gorenstein** and `k` a field. `kA_m` is hereditary
  hence Gorenstein; a general LNA is *not*, so the replicated-algebra route does
  not iterate on LNAs with relations.
- **Cor. 1.11 is not orientation-blind.** `D^b(mod kQ)` does not depend on the
  orientation of `Q`, but `D^b(mod Aus(kQ))` does — `Aus(kA_3)` linear is of type
  `D_6`, alternating is of type `E_6`. Only the *stable* Auslander algebra is
  orientation-independent (Cor. 1.12). Easy to misread from the main theorem.
- The ADE-chain claim "`A(n,3)` is of type `A_1, A_2, A_3, D_4, D_5, E_6, E_7, E_8`"
  holds **only for `n ≤ 8`**; at `n = 9` `A(9,3)` leaves the Dynkin range.

## What it does not give us

- No invariant. The paper never separates two algebras; the only separating
  remark (1.14) is about cancelling fractions in a CY dimension.
- No statement about piecewise heredity, and no contact with quipus.
- No criterion for when two *general* LNAs are derived equivalent — Theorem A
  needs a tilting complex `U` over `B` handed to it, and the paper only ever
  hands it `ZA_n`.

## How it lands on our problem

Two concrete jobs.

1. **Implement Corollary 1.2 as a merge rule on digit strings.** For each `N`,
   for each factorisation `N = m·n` with `1 < m < n`, join
   `str(m+1)*(N-m-1)+"0"*(m-1)` and `str(n+1)*(N-n-1)+"0"*(n-1)`. It is O(1) per
   pair, needs no search, and reaches arbitrarily large `N`. At `N ≤ 10` it
   confirms merges the search already made; above that it is free evidence.
2. **Use the rectangle as the class's normal form.** `A(mn, m+1) ≃ kA_m ⊗ kA_n`
   means the class has a two-dimensional representative. Since
   `C_{X×Y} = C_X ⊗ C_Y` and `Φ_{X×Y} = -Φ_X ⊗ Φ_Y` (Lemma 3.7 of
   arXiv:math/0611201), everything invariant is a tensor product of two `A`-type
   pieces — which is how one *proves* a Coxeter polynomial for the family rather
   than computing it. Ueda's `L(s,t,u)` is the generalisation of this normal form
   to `N` that do not factor.
