# Perverse equivalences, BB-tilting, mutations and applications

**arXiv:** [1001.4765](https://arxiv.org/abs/1001.4765) · S. Ladkani ·
26 Jan 2010 · *read 2026-09-19 from the arXiv PDF*

The same mutation the repo implements, from the other side — and with the thing
arXiv:2112.08129 does not supply: a **necessary and sufficient combinatorial
criterion** for the mutation at a vertex to be a derived equivalence. Plus an
`O(n^2)` formula for the Cartan matrix of the mutated algebra, so the Coxeter
polynomial of a mutation can be computed without performing it.

Sections 4 and 5 are about cluster-tilting objects in 2-Calabi–Yau categories and
are not for us; Sections 1–3 are.

## Set-up (§1.2)

`A = KQ/I`, `k` a vertex of `Q` **without loops**. Let

    L_k = ( P_k  --f-->  ⊕_{j→k} P_j )      P_k in degree -1
    R_k = ( ⊕_{k→j} P_j  --g-->  P_k )      P_k in degree  1

where `f` is induced by the arrows *ending* at `k` and `g` by the arrows
*starting* at `k`; `f` is the minimal left `add(A/P_k)`-approximation of `P_k` and
`g` the minimal right one. Then

    T^-_k = L_k ⊕ (⊕_{i≠k} P_i)      T^+_k = R_k ⊕ (⊕_{i≠k} P_i)

— replace the summand `P_k` of `A` by `L_k` or `R_k`. When `T^±_k` is a tilting
complex, `μ^±_k(A) = End_{D^b(A)}(T^±_k)` is the **positive / negative mutation**
of `A` at `k` (Definition 3.1). `T^+_k` is the one that matches the repo's right
tilting mutation `μ^R_i`: it is built from the arrows **out of** `k`.

## The results we use

### Proposition 2.3 — the gate, as an iff, in paths

> Let `k` be a vertex without loops.
>
> (a) `T^-_k` is a tilting complex **if and only if** for any non-zero linear
> combination `Σ a_r p_r` of paths **starting at `k`** and ending at some vertex
> `i ≠ k`, there is at least one arrow `α` ending at `k` with `Σ a_r α p_r ≠ 0`.
>
> (c) `T^+_k` is a tilting complex **if and only if** for any non-zero linear
> combination `Σ a_r p_r` of paths **starting at some `i ≠ k` and ending at `k`**,
> there is at least one arrow `β` starting at `k` with `Σ a_r p_r β ≠ 0`.

Compare `procedure.isMutable`, which is arXiv:2112.08129's criterion read
exactly: it loops over **single paths** `p : i ⇝ k`, skips those in the ideal,
and returns `False` as soon as one survives whose composite with *every* arrow
out of `k` is in the ideal. **That is Proposition 2.3(c) with the linear
combinations replaced by single paths.** The repo file for arXiv:2112.08129 says
the criterion "rules mutations out; it does not rule them in", and R-005 is 38
"rules" admitted on the strength of exactly that gap. Proposition 2.3(c) closes
it: quantify over the whole span and the condition becomes an equivalence.

Restated so it can be implemented: for each vertex `i ≠ k`, let `V_i` be the span
of the paths `i ⇝ k` modulo `I`, and consider

    V_i  -->  ⊕_{β : k→j}  (span of paths i ⇝ j) / I ,     p ↦ (p·β)_β .

`T^+_k` is tilting **iff this map is injective for every `i ≠ k`**. That is a
rank computation over the field, and `arrowPaths.combination` /
`arrowPaths.isInIdeal` already speak the right language — the repo has carried
coefficients since F-015, so nothing new is needed to express it.

The homological form of the same statement is Proposition 2.1: `T^+_k` is tilting
iff `Hom_{D^b(A)}(R_k[1], P_i) = 0` for all `i ≠ k`; and dually for `T^-_k` with
`Hom(P_i, L_k[-1]) = 0`. Corollary 2.2 gives a third form: `T^-_k` is tilting iff
`ker f ≅ S_k^m` for some `m ≥ 0`, and is a tilting *module* iff `f` is mono.

### Proposition 3.6 — the Cartan matrix of a mutation, in closed form

> Define `n × n` matrices `r^±_k` that are the identity except in row `k`:
>
>     (r^-_k)_{kj} = -δ_{kj} + #{arrows j → k}
>     (r^+_k)_{kj} = -δ_{kj} + #{arrows k → j}
>
> Then, whenever the mutation is defined,
> `C_{μ^-_k(A)} = r^-_k · C_A · (r^-_k)^T` and
> `C_{μ^+_k(A)} = r^+_k · C_A · (r^+_k)^T`.

This is the general Lemma 3.5 — for any tilting complex `T` with summands `T_i`,
`C_{End T} = r C_A r^T` where `r_{ij}` is the class `[T_i] = Σ_j r_{ij}[P_j]` —
specialised to the two-term complexes above.

**Verified against the repo, 2026-09-19.** For every LNA of length 4, 5 and 6 and
every vertex the repo's gate admits, `mutation.quiverMutationAtVertex` produces an
algebra whose `invariants.cartanMatrix` is exactly `r^+_k C (r^+_k)^T` — 171
mutations, no exceptions, with `invariants.cartanMatrix`'s own convention and no
transposing. That also settles the identification: **the repo's right tilting
mutation is Ladkani's `μ^+_k`**, which is the reading Remark 2.4 predicts (`μ^+`
is impossible at a sink, always possible at a source, and `procedure.isMutable`
refuses a vertex with no arrow out and accepts vertex 1 of a line vacuously).

Three consequences worth having:

1. **The Coxeter polynomial along a mutation path costs `O(n^2)` per step.** A
   search that currently rewrites the quiver and recomputes the Cartan matrix can
   instead multiply by `r^±_k`, and only materialise the algebra when it wants to
   read off relations. It also gives an independent check on the rewriting.
2. It is the explicit `P` in "derived equivalent ⟹ Cartan matrices congruent over
   `Z`" (Corollary 3.13 of arXiv:math/0610685) for the mutation case, so every
   invariant in `math-0610685-sheaves-over-finite-posets.md` is automatically
   preserved along a mutation path — a proof that the Coxeter-conjugacy
   refinement is constant on mutation classes, rather than the empirical check.
3. `r^+_k` depends only on the **number of arrows out of `k`**, so two quivers
   that agree on that have Cartan matrices related by the same transformation.

### Proposition 3.2 — mutations undo each other

> If `μ^-_k(A)` is defined then `μ^+_k(μ^-_k(A))` is defined and isomorphic to
> `A`; and symmetrically. If `μ^{BB}_k(A)` is defined then
> `μ^{BB}_k((μ^{BB}_k(A))^{op})` is defined and isomorphic to `A^op`.
>
> At a given vertex, both, exactly one, or neither of `μ^±_k` may be defined.

### Remark 2.4 and Corollary 2.5 — the easy cases and the dual

> If `k` is a **sink**, `T^-_k` is always a tilting complex and `T^+_k` never is.
> If `k` is a **source**, `T^+_k` always is and `T^-_k` never is.
>
> `T^-_k(A)` is a tilting complex for `A` iff `T^+_k(A^{op})` is one for `A^{op}`.

Corollary 2.5 is the clean statement behind `leftQuiverMutationAtVertex` being
"dual → right mutation → dual": left and right mutation at a vertex are the same
operation read in `A^op`, and — importantly — their *legality* transfers too.

## Lemmas worth knowing

- **Definition 1.1 and Proposition 2.8.** The BB-tilting module
  `T^{BB}_k = τ^{-1}S_k ⊕ (⊕_{i≠k}P_i)` is defined iff `Hom_A(DA, S_k) = 0` and
  `Ext^1(S_k,S_k) = 0`. When it is defined, `T^-_k` is a tilting complex and the
  two agree in `D^b(A)`; conversely if `T^-_k` is isomorphic to a tilting
  *module* then `T^{BB}_k` is defined. So APR/BB tilts are the special case of
  this mutation that happens in degree zero.
- **Lemma 2.9 + Remark 2.10.** If `End_A(P_k) ≅ K` — which holds whenever `Q` is
  **acyclic**, or `A` is schurian — then `T^-_k` tilting already implies
  `T^-_k ≅ T^{BB}_k`. Every quiver we mutate before hitting a cycle is acyclic,
  so in our world "negative mutation" and "BB-tilt" coincide.
- **Lemma 2.6.** `T^{BB}_k` is defined iff `P_k` does not occur as a summand of
  `R^0` in a minimal projective resolution `… → R^{-1} → R^0 → 0` of `DA`.
- **Lemma 3.4.** For a quiver without loops or 2-cycles,
  `μ_k(b_Q) = (r^-_k)^T b_Q r^-_k = (r^+_k)^T b_Q r^+_k` where `b_Q` is the
  skew-symmetric matrix of `Q` and `μ_k` is Fomin–Zelevinsky matrix mutation. So
  the `K_0` shadow of an algebra mutation *is* cluster mutation. Useful if the
  repo ever wants to compare its orbits with a cluster-mutation class; not
  useful for merging, since `b_Q` forgets the relations.

## Caveats and limitations

- **`K` algebraically closed** from §3 on (Definition 3.1 onwards). Sections 1–2,
  including Proposition 2.3, do not need it.
- **`k` must have no loops.** Fine for us until a mutation produces one.
- **`T^+_k` is never isomorphic to a tilting module** (Proposition 2.1(d)) —
  `H^1(T^+_k) ≠ 0` always. So the positive mutation is genuinely a complex, and
  there is no module-level shortcut on that side.
- **Mutation gets stuck, and the paper shows it.** Example 3.3: `A = kA_3`
  linearly oriented; at vertex 1 only `μ^+` is defined, at vertex 3 only `μ^-`,
  at vertex 2 both. But for `A' = μ^-_2(A)`, **neither** `μ^-_1(A')` nor
  `μ^+_1(A')` is defined. A vertex that was mutable can stop being so.
- **Proposition 2.3 is a criterion, not a construction.** It tells you whether
  the mutation is legal; it does not give the quiver with relations of
  `μ^±_k(A)`. That is still arXiv:2112.08129's seven steps. Proposition 3.6 gives
  only the Cartan matrix.
- The paper's `μ^±_k` are mutations of **algebras**, not of quivers: two
  different algebras with the same quiver can behave differently at the same
  vertex.

## What it does not give us

- No merge between two named LNAs, and no worked example on a line.
- No invariant that separates.
- Nothing about when a *sequence* of mutations is legal — only one step at a time,
  and Example 3.3 shows legality is not inherited.

## How it lands on our problem

**Replace `procedure.isMutable` with Proposition 2.3(c).** The current gate is the
single-path shadow of it and is only necessary; the paper's version is an
equivalence and is expressible with machinery the repo already has (spans of
paths modulo the ideal, with coefficients). That turns the search's gate from a
filter that must be backed up by a Coxeter-polynomial check (F-016) into a
decision procedure, and removes the mechanism that produced R-005.

Second, **Proposition 3.6 makes the Coxeter polynomial free along a search
path.** `coxeterKey` is currently recomputed from the mutated algebra; with
`C ↦ r^+_k C (r^+_k)^T` it is a rank-one row update, and disagreement between the
two is a bug signal in the rewriting — the same kind of cross-check that E-003
runs between the exact and cheap Cartan matrices.
