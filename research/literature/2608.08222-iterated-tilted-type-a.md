# Enumerating iterated tilted algebras in type A

**arXiv:** [2608.08222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08222) · A. E. Black, J. E. Gordon,
R. Maresca

Read for one thing it is not about: it is the cleanest modern restatement of
**Assem–Happel**, *Generalized tilted algebras of type A_n* (Comm. Algebra 9
(1981)), which is journal-only and pre-arXiv. The paper's own contribution is a
count; the count is a curiosity for us, the classification it quotes is not.

That classification is a **complete, finite, purely local certificate** that an
algebra is derived equivalent to `kA_n`. We have nothing else of that shape.

## What it gives us

### 1. Assem–Happel, restated (Theorem 1.5 + Proposition 1.6)

> **Theorem 1.5** ([AH81]; also Theorem 2.2 of Qiu, *Exchange graphs and
> stability conditions for quivers*, arXiv:1111.1010). Let `A = kQ/I`. Then `A`
> is an iterated tilted algebra of type `A_n` **if and only if** `A` is a *gentle
> tree algebra* with `n` simple modules.

`A = kQ/I` is a **gentle tree algebra** when all four hold:

1. `Q` is a tree (underlying graph — no cycles, oriented or not);
2. every vertex is the source of at most two arrows and the target of at most
   two arrows;
3. for each arrow `α` there is **at most one** arrow `β` with `αβ ∉ I` and at
   most one arrow `γ` with `γα ∉ I`;
4. for each arrow `α` there is **at most one** arrow `δ` with `αδ ∈ I` and at
   most one arrow `ε` with `εα ∈ I`, **and `I` is generated by these monomials**.

Condition 4's last clause is the one that matters to us: `I` is generated by
paths of **length exactly two**. So a gentle tree algebra is gentle, and the
theorem is a two-way street:

- **(merging)** every gentle tree algebra on `n` vertices, whatever its tree and
  whatever its orientation, is derived equivalent to `kA_n` — one class;
- **(separating)** an algebra with any minimal relation of three or more arrows
  is **not** derived equivalent to `kA_n`.

Iterated tilted of type `A_n` and derived equivalent to `kA_n` are the same
condition here: Happel's theorem gives `D^b(A) ≃ D^b(kQ)` for `A` iterated tilted
of type `Q`, and for `Q` Dynkin the converse holds too.

**The separating direction has a second, stronger source.** Schröer–Zimmermann,
*Stable endomorphism algebras of modules over special biserial algebras*
([math/0208150](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0208150), Math. Z. 244 (2003)) prove
that **any algebra derived equivalent to a gentle algebra is gentle** —
gentleness is a derived invariant. (Abstract read; paper not read.) So a
non-gentle algebra is derived equivalent to no gentle algebra at all, not merely
to no `kA_n`. For an LNA with a relation of length ≥ 3 that rules out the whole
gentle world in one line, `kA_n` included.

### 2. The colouring normal form (Proposition 1.6, Lemma 2.2)

A **gentle tree** is a directed tree with in- and out-degree ≤ 2 whose edges are
2-coloured so that any two edges *starting* at a vertex, and any two edges
*ending* at a vertex, get different colours.

> **Proposition 1.6** (Prop. 2.4 of Qiu). `kQ/I` is a gentle tree algebra iff `Q`
> is a gentle tree and `I` is either `I⁺`, generated by all **unicoloured** paths
> of length two, or `I⁻`, generated by all **alternating** paths of length two.

> **Lemma 2.2.** Given a gentle colouring `φ` of a gentle tree `T`, there is
> another gentle colouring `ψ` of the same `T` with `ψ(u,v) = ψ(v,w)` iff
> `φ(u,v) ≠ φ(v,w)`.

Lemma 2.2 is why `I⁺` alone suffices: the involution swaps `I⁺` and `I⁻`, so
every gentle tree algebra is `kT/I⁺` for some gentle tree `T`, unique up to
isomorphism. Practically: to test membership, 2-colour the arrows by the rule
*two composable arrows get the same colour iff their composite is in `I`*
(the paper's own recipe, p. 7), then check the colouring is gentle.

### 3. The count (Theorem 4.15)

Isoclasses of iterated tilted algebras of type `A_n` are in bijection with
non-crossing spanning trees of a convex `(n+1)`-gon up to rotation, counted by
OEIS A296532:

    a(n) = C(3n,n) / ((n+1)(2n+1))                              n even
    a(n) = ( C(3n,n)/(2n+1) + C((3n-1)/2, (n-1)/2) ) / (n+1)     n odd

Useful only as an external check: this is the number of isomorphism classes of
**algebras** in the hereditary class, so it bounds from above anything we count
there. Our `2^(n-2)` gentle LNAs of length `n` (F-019) are a vanishing fraction
of them, which is the expected shape — most members of the class are not LNAs.

## Lemmas worth knowing

- **Lemma 2.1** is the combinatorial engine: any directed tree with in/out-degree
  ≤ 2 admits a labelling `f: E → {±1}` that alternates along composable pairs and
  is constant on the arrows out of (resp. into) a vertex. That is what makes the
  `I⁺ ↔ I⁻` involution exist.
- The last paragraph of the proof of **Theorem 3.4** is a clean proof that two
  gentle tree algebras are isomorphic iff their quivers-with-relations are: the
  `e_x (rad/rad²) e_y` spaces are ≤ 1-dimensional, so an algebra isomorphism is
  forced to be a quiver isomorphism, and `αβ ∈ I₁ ⟺ e_k A₁ e_i = 0` transports
  the ideal. Worth copying if we ever need *isomorphism* rather than *derived
  equivalence* of two of our quivers.

## Caveats and limitations

- **"Type A_n" is not "tree type".** The theorem is only about derived
  equivalence to the *linear* `A_n`. The quipu theorem of arXiv:2305.06642 puts
  an almost-separate LNA into a class of a **tree** `T` that is usually not
  `A_n`, and there is **no** Assem–Happel-style characterisation for a general
  tree type in this paper or, as far as this sweep found, anywhere. So this
  theorem lands on exactly one of our classes.
- **The tree condition is on `Q`, not on the underlying graph of something
  else.** A quiver produced by mutation that has parallel arrows (F-039) is not a
  tree, so it fails condition 1 immediately and the theorem says nothing about
  it — not even that it is outside the class. The certificate is one-sided in
  practice: passing it proves membership, failing it proves non-membership
  *only* when the failure is condition 4 (a relation longer than two arrows,
  which is derived-invariant by the gentle theory), not when the failure is
  condition 1 or 2.
- Companion classifications exist for other types and were **not** read here:
  Assem, *Tilted algebras of type A_n* (1983); Assem–Skowroński, *Iterated tilted
  algebras of type Ã_n* (1987), the cyclic/gentle case; Happel (1987). The Ã_n
  one is where quivers with cycles enter; for type `A_n` the quiver really is a
  tree.
- The count in Theorem 4.15 is of **algebras up to isomorphism**, not of quivers
  and not of anything we enumerate. Do not compare it to class sizes without
  fixing that.

## What it does not give us

No merge outside the hereditary class. Nothing about relations of length ≥ 3
except that they are fatal to type `A_n`. No invariant — it is a membership test
for one specific class, not a separator of two arbitrary ones.

## For LNAs specifically

An LNA is `kA_n/I` on the linear quiver, so conditions 1–3 of *gentle tree
algebra* are automatic (every vertex has one arrow in and one out). Only
condition 4 bites, and it says every relation has exactly two arrows. So:

> **An LNA is derived equivalent to `kA_n` if and only if every one of its
> relations has length 2.**

That is **F-019 with a theorem under it**, including the converse F-019 only
checked by classification (`n = 4..10`). The *forward* direction was already
proved in the repo from operation 2 of `cor:EquivNakayamaAlgebras`; the
*converse* — no LNA with a long relation is in the hereditary class — is what
Theorem 1.5 supplies, uniformly in `n`.

Where it earns its keep is on **mutated** quivers. The search leaves the LNA
shape after one step, and whenever it lands on a `(Q,I)` that is a tree with
in/out-degree ≤ 2 and all relations of length 2, Theorem 1.5 names the class
outright, with no Coxeter polynomial and no further search. That is a cheap
check to run at every node of `search.py` — conditions 1–4 are local.
