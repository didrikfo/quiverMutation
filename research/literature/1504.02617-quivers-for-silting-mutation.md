# Quivers for silting mutation

**arXiv:** [1504.02617](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.02617) · S. Oppermann ·
Adv. Math. 307 (2017) 684–733

The combinatorial rule for Aihara–Iyama silting mutation, on a **dg quiver**.
It is the same seven-step rewrite `procedure.py` implements, lifted to a setting
where the degrees keep the bookkeeping honest — and where **no admissibility
hypothesis is needed at all**.

Read it for three things: it removes the gate, it closes F-002 (the cyclic
case), and it explains, in one line of degrees, *why* arXiv:2112.08129's
procedure sometimes fails to be a derived equivalence.

## The setup

Work with `Λ = kQ` a **non-negatively graded dg quiver algebra** over `R = k^n`:
arrows carry degrees `|φ| ≥ 0` and there is a differential `d`, determined by its
values on arrows and extended by the graded Leibniz rule.

> **Construction 2.2.** Any basic finite-dimensional `A = kQ^{(0)}/(R)` is
> quasi-isomorphic to such a `Λ`: add arrows of degree 1 for a **minimal set of
> relations**, with `dω = ` that relation, so `H^0(kQ^{(1)}) = A`; then add
> arrows of degree 2 killing `H^1`; iterate.

So the dg quiver of an algebra is: the quiver, plus one degree-1 arrow per
minimal relation, plus one degree-2 arrow per relation *between* relations, and
so on — **the minimal projective bimodule resolution, written as a quiver**.

Example 2.3 is the whole idea in miniature: `k[1 →φ 2 →ψ 3]/(ψφ)` becomes the
quiver `1 → 2 → 3` plus `ω : 1 → 3` in degree 1, with `dω = ψφ`.

## Theorem 1.1 — the main statement

> Let `(kQ, d)` be a dg quiver algebra concentrated in non-negative degree, and
> `i` a vertex of `Q` **with no loops of degree 0 attached to it**. Then the
> derived endomorphism ring of the left Aihara–Iyama silting mutation of `kQ` at
> `i` is the mutated quiver `(kM, ∂)` described in Section 3.

One hypothesis: **no degree-0 loop at `i`**. That is all. Compare
`procedure.isMutable`, which additionally refuses a vertex when some nonzero
path into it dies against every arrow out — a condition that exists only because
the *tilting* mutation may not exist. Here the mutation always exists; what
varies is whether the answer is an algebra.

## The rule (Section 3)

`A` := the set of **degree-0 arrows starting at `i`**. `χ(j) = 1` if `j = i`,
else 0.

**Step 1 — rotation.** Raise the degree of every arrow into `i`, lower it for
every arrow out. Arrows in `A` now have degree −1 and are replaced by
`α* : j → i` of degree 0. Otherwise
`‖φ‖ = |φ| − χ(sφ) + χ(tφ)`.

**Step 2 — composition arrows.** For `φ : j → i` and `α : i → j'` in `A`, an
arrow `αφ : j → j'` with `‖αφ‖ = ‖φ‖ − 1`.

**Step 3 — anti-compositions.** For `φ : i → j'` **not** in `A` and `α : i → j`
in `A`, an arrow `φα⁻¹ : j → j'` with `‖φα⁻¹‖ = ‖φ‖ + 1`.

**Step 4 — cycles at `i`.** For `φ : i → i`, `α : i → j'`, `β : i → j` in `A`,
an arrow `αφβ⁻¹ : j → j'` with `‖αφβ⁻¹‖ = ‖φ‖`.

Paths in `M` are composable sequences of arrows of `Q` plus the symbols `α*`,
`α⁻¹`, subject to: an `α ∈ A` is always preceded by some `φ ∈ Q_1`; an `α⁻¹` is
always followed by some `φ ∈ Q_1 \ A`.

**Three operations on paths** (Construction 3.1):
- `red(p)` — drop every path whose **rightmost** arrow is in `A`;
- `p/α` — keep only the paths starting with `α`, and delete that `α`; so
  `p = red(p) + Σ_{α ∈ A} (p/α) α`;
- `dec(p)` — insert `Δ = 1 − Σ_{α ∈ A} α⁻¹α` wherever the path passes through
  `i`, entering along an arrow of `Q_1` and leaving along an arrow of `Q_1 \ A`.

**The differential** (Definition 3.2):

    ∂(φ)        = dec(red(dφ))                         if tφ ≠ i
    ∂(φ)        = Σ_{α∈A} α* αφ − dec(red(dφ))         if tφ = i
    ∂(αφ)       = dec(α red(dφ))
    ∂(α*)       = 0
    ∂(φα⁻¹)     = ∂(φ)α⁻¹ + (−1)^‖φ‖ φα* − (−1)^{χ(tφ)} dec(dφ/α)
    ∂(αφβ⁻¹)    = ∂(αφ)β⁻¹ + (−1)^‖αφ‖ αφβ* − α dec(dφ/β)

**Theorem 3.3 / 8.1:** `∂² = 0` — not obvious, and proved separately.

## The dictionary with arXiv:2112.08129

Every step of our procedure appears here, and the correspondence is exact once
one remembers that a *relation* of ours is a *degree-1 arrow* of Oppermann's.

| 2112.08129 | 1504.02617 |
|---|---|
| step 1, compositions `αβ : h → j` through `i` | step 2, `αφ`, degree `‖φ‖ − 1` |
| step 2, arrows out of `i` flip to `α*` | step 1, rotation |
| step 3, relations out of `i` become arrows `r̄ : i* → k` | step 1 again — a relation *is* a degree-1 arrow out of `i`, so it rotates |
| step 3's cyclic case, one arrow per `α` out of `i` | **step 4**, `αφβ⁻¹` — the case our code does not implement |
| step 4, `Σ_α α* α β = 0` | `∂(φ) = Σ_α α* αφ − …` for `tφ = i` |
| step 5, `r̄ α* = r/α` | the `dec(dφ/α)` term of `∂(φα⁻¹)` |
| step 6/7, relations into and out of `i*` | the remaining terms of `∂`, and the reduction below |

**Reduction.** Examples 4.1 and 4.2 both finish by dividing out a dg ideal
`(ψ, ∂ψ)` where `∂ψ` contains a degree-0 arrow — quasi-isomorphism is preserved,
the arrow and the generator both vanish. That is exactly the note after step 7 in
our summary, and exactly what `reducePathAlgebra` does.

## Example 4.2 — an LNA-shaped worked example

Quiver `1 →α 2 →β 3 →γ 4`, plus `δ` out of 2, with relations `βα` and `γβ`.
Its dg model needs `r, s` in degree 1 (`dr = βα`, `ds = γβ`) **and `x` in degree
2**, with `dx = sα − γr` — because the two relations **overlap in the arrow `β`**.

That is the single most useful line in the paper for us. **Overlapping relations
force generators in degree ≥ 2, and an overlap of one arrow is already enough**
— `βα` and `γβ` share exactly `β`, which is the almost-separate bound of
arXiv:2305.06642, and `x` still appears. The dg quiver is where the overlap
profile `overlap.overlapProfile` computes becomes visible as homological data
rather than as a string statistic. Only an LNA whose relations are pairwise
**disjoint** has a dg model with arrows in degrees 0 and 1 alone.

Mutating at vertex 2 and reducing, the paper's answer is quasi-isomorphic to
`A^op` — with `x` surviving in degree 2 and `∂(x) = sα − sδ⁻¹δα`. So even a
small LNA-like algebra mutates to something that is **not concentrated in degree
0** until it is reduced, and may not reduce to an algebra at all.

## Caveats and limitations

- **The output is a dg algebra, not an algebra.** It is an algebra exactly when,
  after reduction, no arrow of nonzero degree survives and `H^{≠0} = 0`. That
  condition is the honest replacement for `isMutable`: the rewrite is always
  defined; whether it lands in our world is decided afterwards, by degrees. A
  surviving negative-degree arrow is precisely the failure of the tilting
  condition `Hom(T, T[<0]) = 0` of Aihara–Iyama Theorem 2.32.
- **Left mutation only.** The dual statement is the same theorem for `Q^op`, as
  the introduction says. Our `leftQuiverMutationAtVertex` already does
  dual → mutate → dual; the convention must be checked once, not assumed.
- **Signs are load-bearing.** `(−1)^‖φ‖` and `(−1)^{χ(tφ)` in `∂(φα⁻¹)` are not
  cosmetic; `∂² = 0` needs Section 8. Our set-of-paths model could not record
  coefficients at all (F-015), and this is the same lesson in graded form.
- `red` and `/α` are defined on **linear combinations of paths**, and `dec`
  introduces `Δ = 1 − Σ α⁻¹α`, which expands a single path into many. Any
  implementation must carry linear combinations throughout — `arrowPaths` plus
  coefficients, not sets.
- The paper's own applications (Sections 5, 6) are to **Ginzburg algebras** and
  higher quivers with potential — Calabi–Yau, cyclic, and of infinite global
  dimension. Nothing there transfers to LNAs; skip them.
- No statement about *when* the mutated dg algebra is formal, and no
  characterisation of which vertices give algebras. The rule computes; it does
  not predict.

## What it does not give us

No derived invariant. No merge theorem. No answer to whether two LNAs are
derived equivalent — it is machinery for generating one side of such a proof.

## For LNAs specifically

Three things to take.

1. **F-002 is closed in principle.** Step 4 is the cyclic case our
   implementation omits, stated here with degrees, so the second worked example
   of arXiv:2112.08129 can be checked against `αφβ⁻¹` rather than guessed at.
2. **The dg model of an LNA is small and explicit.** Degree-0 arrows = the `n−1`
   arrows of the line; degree-1 arrows = the minimal relations; degree-2 arrows =
   one per pair of relations sharing at least one arrow (Example 4.2); degree-`m`
   arrows = one per chain of `m` relations each overlapping the next. That is
   exactly Bardzell's indexing by `m`-ambiguities — see
   `2312.14699-hochschild-monomial-bardzell.md`, where the same combinatorics is
   set up independently — so the dg quiver of an LNA is read straight off the
   overlap profile, with **no** arrows above degree 1 when the relations are
   pairwise disjoint.
3. **A principled gate.** Instead of `isMutable` refusing a vertex, mutate
   always, then reduce, then test whether anything of nonzero degree survives.
   That test is decidable on a finite dg quiver and is equivalent to the tilting
   condition, where the present gate is only necessary.
