# Non-piecewise hereditary Nakayama algebras

**arXiv:** [2310.08346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08346)

Asks when `kA_n / J` is piecewise hereditary, and gives criteria showing it often
is not. For us this is the tool that says **a class is not a quipu class**, with
no search.

## Why it matters here

An algebra is *piecewise hereditary* if `D^b(Λ) ≃ D^b(H)` for a hereditary abelian
category `H`. By Happel's classification such an `H` is either the module category
of a hereditary algebra or derived equivalent to a canonical algebra. So:

- not piecewise hereditary ⟹ derived equivalent to **no** hereditary algebra ⟹ in
  no quipu class;
- piecewise hereditary but not of tree type ⟹ should be of **canonical** type, and
  the Coxeter polynomial names the weights.

That is the trichotomy of H-006, and it named every n = 9 class (F-011).

## Results we use

### Counts (from the introduction)

- Every `kA_n / J` with **n ≤ 8** is piecewise hereditary.
- Among the 1430 quotients of `kA_9` there is **precisely one** that is not,
  namely `(**)`: relations 1→4, 3→6, 4→7, 6→9. In our notation `3033030`.

Both are used as tests: our criteria must certify nothing below length 9 and
exactly that one algebra at length 9. This is the strongest check we have on the
implementation.

### Proposition A9 (`proposition:A9extended`)

At least 9 vertices, and relations `α` (leftmost) and `β` overlapping by **≥ 2
arrows**, with:

1. no relation starting at the vertex directly before `α`'s start and ending at or
   before the vertex directly after `β`'s start;
2. no relation ending at the vertex directly after `β`'s end and starting at or
   after the vertex directly before `α`'s end;
3. some relation of length **≥ 3** lying entirely before `β` (may share a vertex);
4. some relation of length ≥ 3 lying entirely after `α` (may share a vertex).

Then not piecewise hereditary. Loosely: *an overlapping pair flanked by a long
relation on each side.* Implemented as `failsFlankedPairCriterion`.

### Proposition A13 (`proposition:A13tworelations`)

Relations `α` (leftmost) and `β` overlapping by **≥ 6 arrows**, with at least two
vertices between the two starts and at least two between the two ends, and no
relation from the first-or-second vertex before `β`'s start to the first-or-second
vertex after `α`'s end. Then not piecewise hereditary.

Smallest instance: `A_13` with 1→10 and 4→13. Implemented as
`failsBigOverlapCriterion`.

### Corollary `removevertex`

If `Λ` is piecewise hereditary, so is `Γ` obtained by deleting a vertex `i` —
adding a composite arrow if `i` has both an arrow in and out, and **extending** the
relations that start or end at `i`.

Used contrapositively, and downward: if some one-vertex deletion of `Λ` is
certified, so is `Λ` (F-012, `notPiecewiseHereditaryByDeletion`).

### Corollary `introducevertex` — and why we do not use it directly

The same result read forwards: add a vertex, with constrained choices of relations,
and non-piecewise-heredity is preserved. **Extensions are not unique** — many
algebras of length `n+1` restrict to a given one of length `n` — so going up means
enumerating a branching set of possibilities, while going down is one deterministic
algebra per vertex. Hence the downward recursion.

### Other derived equivalences stated

- `corollary:lengthtworelations`: relations of length two do not affect the
  derived equivalence type. (Agrees with arXiv:2305.06642.)
- `proposition:doubleMutation`: certain tilting mutations of Nakayama algebras
  give new Nakayama algebras. **Not yet read in detail — likely relevant to the
  move rules of `lnaMoves`, and worth doing so.**

## Caveats and limitations

- The criteria are **sufficient, not a characterisation**. A negative result means
  only that these criteria say nothing. All our counts of non-piecewise-hereditary
  algebras are therefore lower bounds (H-004).
- Deleting a vertex preserves piecewise heredity **only** — not the derived
  equivalence class, the Coxeter polynomial, or the quipu. The certificate is
  purely negative and must never be used to merge two classes.
- The paper notes that two LNAs can share a Coxeter polynomial with one piecewise
  hereditary and the other not (its `remark:Coxeter`), so the Happel–Seidel style
  argument via a *list* of Coxeter polynomials cannot be relied on in general.
- The introduction's framing is worth keeping in mind: the scarcity of
  non-piecewise-hereditary examples at small `n` is an **artefact of the sizes
  computer search can reach**, not a fact about Nakayama algebras.
