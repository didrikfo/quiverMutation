# A categorification of non-crossing partitions

**arXiv:** [1310.1907](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1907) · A. Hubery, H. Krause ·
**v2**, 6 Jun 2015 (final version, accepted by JEMS) · *read 2026-11-26 (round 139,
scholar) from the v2 LaTeX source `catnc.tex`; every number below was matched in the
compiled v2 PDF, because the source's `\begin{appendix}` turns section 8, 9 counters
into A, B (the record's E-308 calls A.7 by its label `co:complete`)*

What it is for: exceptional sequences in `mod H`, `H` a hereditary artin algebra, read
off the Grothendieck group `K_0(H)` with its (non-symmetric) Euler form, which the
paper axiomatises as a *generalised Cartan lattice* `(Γ, E)`. Its main theorem is
combinatorial (subcategories `C(X)` = non-crossing partitions). **We use none of the
combinatorics.** We use it as a citable, arXiv-readable home for four standard facts
about exceptional objects over a Dynkin path algebra, and for a quotation of Happel's
Dynkin recognition theorem. Those are what E-287 / E-289 / E-328 / F-056 lean on
(object lists of `D^b(kD_n)`, generation of `D^b` by a full exceptional sequence).

Setting: `H` hereditary artin, `mod H` finitely generated modules; exceptional module
= indecomposable with `Ext^1(X,X) = 0`; exceptional sequence `(X_1..X_r)`:
`Hom(X_i,X_j) = 0 = Ext^1(X_i,X_j)` for `i > j`; *complete* when `r = rk K_0(H)`.
`C(X)` = smallest full subcategory containing the `X_i`, closed under kernels,
cokernels, extensions. Everything is in `mod H`, **not** in `D^b`: shifts are ours.

## Results we use

- **Cor. A.7** (label `co:complete`, source l. 1444). For an exceptional sequence `X`
  in `mod H`: `mod H = C(X, X^⊥)`; in particular `X` complete `⟺ C(X) = mod H ⟺
  X^⊥ = 0`. *Use:* a complete exceptional sequence generates `mod H` under
  kernels, cokernels, extensions, hence generates `D^b(H)` as a thick subcategory
  (that last step is ours, elementary: `D^b(H)` is generated by `mod H`, and
  kernels/cokernels/extensions in `mod H` are cones and summands in `D^b`).
- **Cor. A.8** (`co:extend`, l. 1453; HK credit Crawley-Boevey 1992 Lemma 1). An
  incomplete exceptional sequence extends at any position `i`, uniquely if the
  result is complete.
- **Thm 4.7** (`th:braid`, l. 883; credited to Crawley-Boevey 1992, Ringel 1994).
  The braid group `B_n`, `n = rk K_0`, acts transitively on complete exceptional
  sequences. *This is the statement the record calls "CB1992 / Ringel 1994",
  UNVERIFIED in their primaries; HK give "an alternative proof" (l. 881): Prop. 4.6
  plus transitivity on factorisations of the Coxeter element (Thm 3.3,
  `th:IS2010`, Igusa-Schiffler, quoted, primary not read).*
- **Thm 4.9** (`th:Gabriel`, l. 906). `H` of Dynkin type (or rank 2): `X ↦ [X]` is
  a bijection exceptional modules ↔ positive real roots; and `H` is
  representation-finite ⟺ Dynkin ⟺ every indecomposable is exceptional.
  *Use:* for `kD_n`, the indecomposables are exactly the exceptional modules and
  number `n(n-1)` (positive roots of `D_n`, a standard count not in the paper);
  with "every indecomposable of `D^b(H)` is a shifted module" (Happel 1988, **not**
  in this paper, UNVERIFIED here) this is E-287's object list of `n(n-1)` up to shift.
- **Prop. 4.6** (`pr:exceptional-ncp`, l. 858). Exceptional sequences of length `r`
  in `mod H` ↔ real exceptional sequences in `K_0(H)` (up to sign) ↔ reflection
  factorisations of non-crossing partitions; `B_r`-equivariant.
- **Thm 7.4** (`th:Happel`, l. 1245; HK cite Happel 1987 *Comment. Math. Helv.* 62
  Thm 5.12, primary **not read**). `A` basic, simply connected, finite-dimensional
  over an **algebraically closed** `k`: `A ~der kQ` with `Q` Dynkin ⟺ `K_0(A) ≅
  K_0(kQ)` isometric (non-symmetric Euler forms). *Use (round 139):* with an
  explicit isometry, a third route to `Λ(n,n-1) = kA_n/rad^{n-1} ~der kD_n`.
- **Thm A.4** (`th:subcat`, l. 1397). For `C ⊆ mod H` closed under kernels,
  cokernels, extensions: reflective ⟺ coreflective ⟺ `mod B` via a finite
  homological epimorphism ⟺ `C(X)` ⟺ `Y^⊥` ⟺ `^⊥Z` for exceptional sequences;
  lengths `rk K_0(B)` and `rk K_0(H) − rk K_0(B)`. The thick-subcategory
  classification the record attributes to "Bruning Th. 5.1" (secondary number) is
  the derived-category relative of this; **this is not Bruning's theorem** and
  does not replace it for non-module thick subcategories of `D^b`.

## Lemmas worth knowing

- Lemma 4.3 (`le:rigid-dim-vector`, Kerner): rigid modules with the same class in
  `K_0` are isomorphic. Prop. 4.4: an exceptional class is a real root.
- Cor. 4.8: a real root is a real Schur root iff `s_a` is a non-crossing partition —
  depends on `K_0(H)` only.
- Thm 3.15: `(Γ,E)` Dynkin ⟺ Coxeter element of finite order ⟺ NC finite.
- §6 (l. 1119): `[τX] = c[X]`, τ acts on exceptional sequences by conjugation by the
  Coxeter element `c` — the K0 shadow our `Φ` computations use.

## Caveats

- **Hereditary only.** Nothing here applies to an LNA directly; every use goes
  through a derived equivalence to `kQ` first (for `B_n`: Fosse Cor. 4.6, then
  `Λ(n,n-1) ~ kD_n`).
- **Modules, not complexes.** "Rigid indecomposable object of `D^b(kD_n)` = shifted
  exceptional module" and "the summands of a silting object order into an
  exceptional sequence" (E-308's two sub-steps) are **not** in this paper. The first
  follows from Thm 4.9 + Happel's hereditary `D^b` description (not read); the
  second stays UNVERIFIED.
- Thm 7.4 is Happel's, quoted with `\qed`; its hypotheses (alg. closed `k`, simply
  connected) are stricter than ours elsewhere. Tree algebras (every LNA) are
  simply connected — standard, not checked in a source this round.
- Numbering trap: the label-order count of the LaTeX gives 8.x / 9.x for the
  appendix; the PDF says A.x / B.x. Cite A.x.

## What it does not give us

No statement about Nakayama algebras, tilting complexes, silting mutation or the
silting order; no derived invariant beyond the isometry class of `K_0`; nothing
for non-Dynkin types that our classes need (the `[2,3,r]` / sheaf / Fuchsian types
are outside its finite cases except via the general Cartan-lattice statements).
