## Strengths

1. **It runs unattended and reliably.** 44 rounds, every one pushed, no lost
   work. The "decide and record" rule (step 0.5) kept it moving without the
   human.
2. **Refereeing is real work.** Referees re-run the reproduction commands,
   rebuild examples by hand, and grep the record. Sample: the round-043 review
   of E-145 reproduced every count, rebuilt four steps from arrows and
   relations, and listed five specific items required for acceptance.
3. **It finds real defects.** The `reduceAgainstPivots` bug (round 015-017) was
   found and fixed by the workshop. E-145 (round 043) found key-preserving,
   gate-admitted, non-tilting steps inside LNA key classes, which undercuts the
   justification of `coxeterGuard` in `quivermutation/search.py` (below).
4. **It corrects itself quickly.** Overclaims from capped samples were
   overturned within one to three rounds: E-129 by E-131, E-132 by E-134,
   E-134 by E-137, E-140 by E-145, E-143 by E-146.
5. **Negative results are recorded honestly**, with limits ("not found
   (capped)", "unsupported, not refuted").
6. **Cheap conferences** (haiku) give a useful convergence signal.

## Weaknesses

1. **Review has no teeth.** "Minor revision" is the default verdict and the
   chair turns it into "accepted with qualifications". The referee's
   *Required for acceptance* items become a "Limits:" sentence in the E-entry
   instead of being done. The revise loop exists on paper (`Awaiting
   revision`) but ran three times in 33 rounds. Several of the fast reversals
   above are exactly the unchecked required items (matched-budget re-runs,
   wider samples).
2. **Nothing is consolidated.** The workshop writes E-entries and never turns
   them into findings, retractions or status changes. 95 experiments, 0
   findings. Hypothesis status lines became run-on logs. No one in the
   procedure is responsible for asking "what do we now know?".
3. **Consequences for the project are not escalated.** E-145 says the
   Coxeter-key guard admits gate-admitted, non-tilting steps at n = 7 (classes
   1, 2), on walks that reach those parents along tilting steps. The docstring
   of `mutationSearchDepthFirst` says the guard "is what makes the walk a walk
   in one derived class", and class merges in `FINDINGS.md` rest on guarded
   walks. The workshop recorded E-145 as "the key guard is no evidence for
   H-015 off J = 0 steps" and moved on: H-015 is still **SUPPORTED**, and
   nobody asked whether any recorded class merge passes through a J != 0 step.
   (Caveat: "Cartan incongruent" here means incongruent under the step's own
   map, E-136, so it does not by itself prove the child is outside the class.)
4. **The agenda collapsed onto one thread.** Since round 022 every slot went
   to T5 or S-1. T5 is a deep question about the tool (why the gate admits
   non-tilting steps); its sub-questions (d_i, out(i), Q(x), c_2, orbit
   relation) each produced a law on a capped sample, then a counterexample.
   Meanwhile H-021, H-020, H-017 and H-010 were dropped without a decision.
   Conferences reinforce this: all six personas read the same `STATE.md` and
   five of six named the same question each time.
5. **The optional question took a third of the slots.** S-1 was added as "not
   a priority". It got one or more slots in 9 of the 15 ordinary rounds since round 025,
   mostly because the maverick's archetype fits it and the call rules only say
   "never in place of a revision or the agenda".
6. **`STATE.md` is append-only in practice.** The thread list was last
   rewritten in round 006; each chair adds a "Round NNN updates (supersede
   everything below)" section. A persona reading it first gets 2006-era thread
   text before the current state.
7. **Questions to the human are noise.** Every round asks "keep the agenda?"
   and "overnight?" and the answer is always the recommended default. The one
   question that needed the human (the arXiv PDFs) was buried among them.
8. **Literature is blocked.** `arxiv.org` is denied by the environment's
   network policy, so Aihara-Iyama 2.31/2.32 and CHZ 3.6, which T5 leans on,
   are cited from memory, flagged UNVERIFIED, for 40 rounds.
9. **Repository bloat.** Rounds 038 and 039 committed about 3 MB of raw data
   each.

## Other observations

- The workshop's best results came when a persona built an **independent**
  check of someone else's claim (skeptic rebuilding E-140's setting in
  E-145; referee rebuilding E-134's path with `tiltingPlus` in E-137). Asking
  for that explicitly is cheap and pays.
- Each law found on walks (d_i = 2, out(i) = 3, "J != 0 moves the key",
  orbit relation) held on class 0 at small n and failed in classes 1, 2 or at
  larger depth. Class 0 is not representative; a claim tested only there
  should say so in its title.
- The chair's decisions are uniformly conservative ("none", "keep"). That is
  what step 0.5 asks for, but together with weakness 4 it means no chair
  ever changes direction.

## Changes made

1. **Same-round response to review** (`ROUND.md` step 3.5). After the reviews,
   each author whose review has *Required for acceptance* items is briefed
   once more, in the same round, to do the items that fit the limits and to
   say which do not. Promotion uses the answered version. "Revise" (next
   round) is kept for major revisions. Costs one more subagent call per
   submission with required items; expected to remove most of the fast
   reversals.
2. **Scope is part of the claim** (submission template, promotion rules). A
   submission states its scope (n, classes, caps on depth/time/sample) in a
   *Scope* line; a law found on a capped sample, or on class 0 only, must say
   so in its title, and the chair copies the scope into the E-entry title.
3. **The ledger** (`ROUND.md`, conference step 3). At every conference the
   chair, before the agenda, goes through the E-entries since the last
   conference and: proposes or writes F-entries for results that are now
   solid, R-entries for beliefs that fell, and a status decision for every
   hypothesis touched; rewrites touched status lines as one short sentence
   (history moves into the body). Recorded in `proceedings.md` under
   *Ledger*.
4. **Consequences for the record** (`ROUND.md` step 4). When an accepted
   result contradicts a finding, a hypothesis status, or a claim in library
   code or docstrings, the chair says so in the proceedings and the digest
   and opens a thread for it; it is not enough to note it in the E-entry.
5. **Breadth** (`ROUND.md` step 1, conference brief). If an open thread has
   had no assignment for 8 or more rounds, one slot in each ordinary round
   goes to a dormant thread or to an explicit decision to close it (as a
   short note). Suggested questions get at most one slot, and not in two
   consecutive rounds. Conference personas also name one question outside
   the current top thread.
6. **`STATE.md` is rewritten, not appended.** No "Round NNN updates" sections;
   threads list current text only; dormant threads marked as such; 120 lines.
7. **Fewer, better questions for the human.** Default decisions ("keep the
   agenda", "no overnight") are recorded as decisions, not asked. A question
   goes to the human only if the chair would decide differently with the
   human's input, or if only the human can do it.
8. **No raw data over 200 KB in commits.** Large outputs are summarised and
   regenerated from the committed script.
9. **A special request for round 045** in `STEERING.md`: rewrite `STATE.md`
   from scratch under the new rules, run the first ledger pass over
   E-053..E-147 for H-015, and open a thread for the guard audit (is any
   recorded class merge carried by a J != 0 step; what does a `tiltingPlus`
   or J = 0 guard cost).

## Not changed, and why

- **Personas, models, researchers_per_round.** The personas do distinct work
  and the referees are good; the problems are in what happens after review.
- **Conference cadence (every 4).** Kept; conferences now also carry the
  ledger, which gives them more to do than the agenda.
- **Command limit (10 min).** Kept. Overnight proposals still go to
  `OVERNIGHT.md`; the human runs them.
