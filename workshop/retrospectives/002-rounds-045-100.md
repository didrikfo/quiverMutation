## Part 1 · The longer lines of the research

### Line A · Does the key-guarded search stay in one derived class? (T10, T5; H-015)

*About 60 % of the period's work.*

- **Rounds 045-051.** Following retrospective 1, H-015 went to OPEN. The
  key guard is not a tilting test (E-147, E-151).
- **Rounds 049-063.** The workshop then asked whether the 25 children that
  fail `tiltingPlus` are nevertheless class members. It found 25 of 25
  joined to an LNA by J = 0 paths (E-157, E-160, E-163). The question
  narrowed to the premise that **J = 0** steps are derived equivalences.
  - The theory part of this went well. The step-7 kernel was identified
    with Oppermann's (E-193, E-199). The Oppermann hypotheses were read in
    the LaTeX (E-189, E-214). Cancellation was proved as a
    quasi-isomorphism (E-235), and Anick's Lemma A was proved directly when
    its citation could not be verified (E-237, E-240). The F/R convention
    was matched to the literature (E-252, E-258).
  - Along the way it found a **real defect in the library**: a cyclic D(i)
    breaks step 7, with an explicit n = 4 counterexample (E-211). The fix
    is the `CyclicDescendantsError` refusal (E-222, E-223). This is the
    period's clearest gain for the project.
- **Rounds 057-100.** The empirical half went badly. The workshop tried to
  test the premise with join searches: does a key-equal pair join by J = 0
  moves? Over 40 rounds it ran this at 4 + 4, 5 + 5, 6 + 6, 6 + 8 and
  7 + 7, on n = 7, 9 and 10 pairs (E-175, E-178, E-219, E-224, E-226,
  E-250, E-255, E-268).
  - Each NO was later found to have no power. Equivalent controls also
    missed, or the miss was a statement about the move set rather than
    about J. Round 093 finally wrote this down (E-255, E-256).
  - The right test had been named at round 073 (E-212: "an independent
    inequivalence certificate with power") and at every conference from
    080 on.
  - Large runs did not help. The overnight 7 + 7 join came back NO, and
    the next round showed it tested nothing about J = 0 (E-250).
- **Where it stands.** The theory is close to a proof. Under the J = 0
  premise, every class merge the search records is supported. Whether the
  search ever *leaves* a class through a J ≠ 0 step is not settled, and
  join searches cannot settle it.
- **Verdict.** The theoretical sub-line deserves F-entries now (see part
  2), and the empirical sub-line should stop.

### Line B · Do Cartan-level invariants separate key-equal classes? (T3/T8)

- **Rounds 063-086.** Every invariant tried came back null: SNF(xC + Cᵀ),
  Z-conjugacy of Φ, piece invariants, definite isometry and congruence
  (E-209, E-215, E-232, E-242). The reason was that no pair was known to
  be *inequivalent* while agreeing on key and profile, so there was no
  real NO control.
- **Side result: periodic does not mean FCY.** Φ periodic does **not**
  give fractionally Calabi–Yau: rank growth of ν^k P was shown in several
  groups, and the verdict is constant on every group swept at n = 10, 11
  and 12 (E-245, E-246, E-253). The literature does not state this
  (E-251).
- **Rounds 094-099.** The line turned once the maverick built the missing
  test set: 8 certified-inequivalent pairs that are equal on profile and
  probes (E-259). The discriminant form q_S then separates 7 of them
  (E-265). An exhaustive search of the Φ-commutant lattice shows the
  eighth is not Z-congruent either (E-269).
- **Where it stands.**
  - This is the first Cartan-level NO with demonstrated power.
  - Its necessity step ("derived equivalent ⇒ Φ-equivariantly
    Z-congruent") has no matched source (E-267, round-100 maverick).
  - The bound in E-269 is a floating-point majorant.
- **Verdict.** This is the best line of the period, and it got there by
  building a test set before testing. It needs the power control the
  round-100 agenda puts first, and a source or proof of the necessity
  step. After that it is a finding.

### Line C · H-010, overlap reducible only at an end (T7)

- **Rounds 059-070.**
  - The single-mutation rule and the Catalan factorisation were **proved**
    for right mutation (E-187), relative to the repo's `procedure`.
  - Left mutation was proved as its mirror (E-205), with a machine check
    per vertex for n = 4..11.
  - The printed steps were matched to arXiv:2112.08129 (E-208).
- **Rounds 081-099.** Two-bystander cells at k = 3, 4 and 5 (E-230,
  E-241, E-260, E-271). Each was one pair shape with weak controls; E-271's
  shape could not have failed.
- **Verdict.** E-187 and E-205 are F-entry material and have been since
  round 070. The bystander sweeps add little per round; one round with
  controls that can fail, then a decision, is enough.

### Line D · H-020 rule table as a theorem (T4)

- H1 was checked to widths 9–11 and lengths up to 14 (E-165, E-188,

### Why the conferences changed no status

No conference after round 045 changed a status: 14 ledgers, 048 to 100,
closed three threads and wrote nothing into `research/`. Four causes, in
order of weight:

1. **The workshop works on questions that are not in the ledger.**
   - The live questions of the period were:
     - the J = 0 premise;
     - "Φ periodic ⇒ FCY";
     - Candidate C (derived classes inside a periodic key group);
     - "Cartan-level invariants are complete for key-equal LNAs at
       n ≤ 12";
     - the T7 bystander rule.
   - None of them is an H-entry. They live as prose inside `STATE.md`
     threads.
   - The ledger can only change the status of an H-entry or write an F-
     or R-entry. So it had nothing it could move. In 100 rounds the
     workshop has never written a hypothesis.
2. **The bar for an F-entry is never met, because every claim is
   narrowed.**
   - "Several E-entries, independent checks, stated scope" is a good bar.
   - However, the conference chair never names a candidate and asks what
     is missing. So the missing check never becomes an assignment.
   - E-187/E-205 (a proof), E-245/E-253 (periodic ⇏ FCY) and E-211 (the
     cyclic-D(i) counterexample, already acted on in code) would each
     pass with one more round of work.
3. **The conference personas echo `STATE.md`.**
   - All six read the same board and the last four digest entries, on the
     cheapest model.
   - Five or six of six named the same "most promising question" (a NO
     with power on the J = 0 premise) at 080, 084, 088, 092 and 100. At
     096 they split 3/3 between that question and the E-259 pairs. At
     every conference from 084 on, five or six named the J = 0 premise as
     the weakest claim.
   - The agenda was re-proposed almost unchanged six times.
4. **The default is conservative.**
   - Step 0.5 says to take "the more conservative option".
   - The ledger allows "no change, with a reason per hypothesis".
   - The reasons were the same sentence each time ("premise untested").
     A ledger that may always say "no change" will.

### Other observations

- **Test design was the real bottleneck, not compute.** Line A spent
  about 25 slots on searches whose NO could not mean what was wanted. The
  workshop has no rule saying "before running it, show the method can
  return NO on a case where NO is the truth". The skeptic did this
  informally (097: "state beforehand what a hit and a miss mean"), and it
  worked.
- **Self-correction still works and is still needed.** About one result in
  ten was corrected later:
  - E-204's sentence, by E-234;
  - E-229, by E-233;
  - E-262, by E-266;
  - the n = 11/n = 10 label of E-245/E-246;
  - the dispatcher's own 7 + 7 README, by E-250.
  All were caught within one to eight rounds, by a persona re-checking
  rather than by review.
- **Literature unblocked well.** Once the human supplied the LaTeX and
  opened arXiv, the scholar ran the following and fixed several citations:
  - a citation audit (E-169, E-171);
  - checks against the printed steps (E-208);
  - queries for prior work (E-251, E-257, E-261);
  - a 28-e-print search for Nikulin (E-267).
  When a citation could not be found, the theorist proved the lemma
  (E-240). Literature work so far has been reactive, one question at a
  time; nobody has surveyed what is known about Line B's invariants.
- **The rota and the 3-slot round are fine.** Personas are distinct, and
  referees re-run commands. Review is honest but rarely blocks: no reject
  in 222 reviews over 100 rounds.
- **Environment.** Round 091 lost re-runs to a command-classifier outage.
  Nothing else was lost; every round pushed.

## The revised setup

The human's plan (STEERING, 2026-10-26) is adopted as written. Its open
names are settled as **phase** (4 rounds) and **cycle** (planning step,
5 phases, cycle retrospective). The procedure is in the new
[`CYCLE.md`](../CYCLE.md), and `ROUND.md` points to it.

| | rounds (cycle 1) | focus | its conference |
|---|---|---|---|
| planning step | before 101 (not a round) | every persona writes a loose, non-binding plan; the chair merges them into `cycles/001/plan.md` and writes the H-entries the plan relies on | — |
| phase 1, **literature** | 101-104 | find and read papers (arXiv), check hypotheses, inconclusive experiments and UNVERIFIED citations against them, cross-reference; computation only to check a published statement | 104: what the literature changed, per thread |
| phases 2-4, **research** | 105-116 | as before | 108, 112, 116: ledger + agenda |
| phase 5, **backlog** | 117-120 | the research backlog: open experiments, unresolved items, inconclusive results, UNVERIFIED citations, pending consequences; settle, retire or convert each | 120: the cycle's closing ledger |
| cycle retrospective | after 120 (not a round) | by the dispatcher session, like this one; may change the format itself | — |

Changes from this retrospective, inside that frame:

1. **Live questions become H-entries** (cause 1).
   - At each planning step, the chair writes every question the plan
     works on as an H-entry, with a falsifiable statement and a status, or
     names the existing H-entry it belongs to.
   - A thread in `STATE.md` must name its H-entry.
   - For cycle 1, these are the J = 0 premise, Φ periodic ⇒ FCY, and
     Cartan-level completeness.
2. **The ledger names its best candidate** (cause 2).
   - At every conference the chair names the strongest F-entry candidate
     and the strongest R-entry candidate since the last conference.
   - For each one, the chair either writes it or states the one check
     that blocks it. That check becomes an assignment in the next
     ordinary round.
   - "No change" is allowed only with that check named.
3. **A "Power" line in every submission** (test design).
   - For any experiment that can return NO, the submission states the
     control on which the method does return NO (or does fail), or says
     there is none.
   - A NO without such a control is recorded as "no power" in its title.
4. **`STATE.md` has a byte limit, checked mechanically** (change 6
   failed).
   - The limits are under 12 000 bytes and each thread entry under 600
     characters.
   - *Standing facts* covers only the current phase.
   - History goes to the cycle's `phase-N.md` summary instead.
   - `ROUND.md` step 5 runs a check that fails the round if `STATE.md` is
     over the limit.
5. **Conferences run on the researcher model**, and each persona briefs
   from its own notebook and its own phase's submissions before reading
   `STATE.md` (cause 3). The conference model changes from haiku to
   sonnet: about $1-2 more per conference, 5 conferences per cycle.
6. **"Approve the agenda" is no longer asked.** The agenda is the chair's.
   The human edits `STEERING.md` or the cycle plan when they want a change.
   The planning step and the cycle retrospective are where the human is
   asked, once each.
7. **Pending consequences are backlog items.**
   - A consequence for library code or docstrings that is still open at
     phase 5 must be done or explicitly declined there.
   - The first one is the `mutationSearchDepthFirst` docstring.
8. **The breadth slot applies in research phases only.** The literature
   and backlog phases already range across all threads.

Not changed: personas, the roster, `researchers_per_round` (3), one
referee, the same-round response, the 10-minute limit, decide-and-record,
S-1's slot cap (research phases), the conference every fourth round, and
the 8-hour schedule.
