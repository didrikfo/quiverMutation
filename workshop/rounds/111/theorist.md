## Claim

(1) M2. Letters of kM (printed by `theorist_m2dtheta.py`): bbar (deg 1, d = c v), c (0, d = 0), fbar = m(.,f,.) (1, d = u v), u = m(a,f,.) (0, d = 0), v = a* (0, d = 0), theta1 = f a^-1 (2, d = theta2 v - v fbar), theta2 = a f a^-1 (1, d = v u). Let I be the dg ideal generated by theta1, theta2 and their differentials. Then kM -> N = kM/I is a quasi-isomorphism, and N = T(bbar,c,fbar,u,v)/(v u, v fbar) as a graded algebra. It does not claim: that N or kM is quasi-isomorphic to a finite-dimensional algebra (kM has H^1 != 0 from the free f), anything for M4/M5, or the Hom statement (3) of E-290.

(2) Degree-0 loop. Take a degree-0 loop g at i, d g = 0, w(g) = 3 (the only weight in 1..3 and 0 for which the E-284 engine runs: w(g) = 1, 0 trip its positivity assertion). The printed letters have the same differentials as M2 (fbar -> u v, theta1 -> theta2 v + v fbar, theta2 -> v u up to sign) with u = m(a,g,.) in degree -1. H(I) = 0 in all 52 cells to weight 12. Since the proof of (1) never uses degrees, it covers this model verbatim: the cancellation holds. So "no loop of degree 0 at i" in Oppermann's Def. 3.2 / Thm 7.5 is not needed for Prop. 7.6's cancellation in the monomial-pivot situation; what a degree-0 loop breaks is a different hypothesis (the algebra kM has a generator u of degree -1, so it is not non-negatively graded; I did not locate which later step needs that). If a library step ever met a degree-0 loop it would be refused anyway (E-211, arrowPaths loop check).

## Evidence

Proof of (1). Put g(bbar) = g(fbar) = g(theta1) = 1 and g = 0 on c, u, v, theta2, extended additively to words. From the printed d: d bbar = c v (g 1 -> 0), d fbar = u v (1 -> 0), d theta1 = theta2 v - v fbar (g 1 -> 0 and 1 -> 1), d theta2 = v u (0 -> 0). So d never raises g (machine-checked on every letter; Leibniz extends it). Filter F_p = span of words with g <= p. In a cell (source, target, weight, degree) the words are finitely many (every v, weight -1, is followed by a letter of weight >= 3, or ends the word), so F is finite, exhaustive, and the spectral sequence converges with no argument. I is generated by theta1, theta2, v fbar, v u (theta2 v lies in (theta2)), all g-homogeneous, so I is a g-graded subspace, F_p I = I intersect F_p, and gr(kM -> N) = ((kM, d0) -> (kM, d0)/I) with d0 the g-preserving part: d0 bbar = d0 fbar = d0 c = d0 u = d0 v = 0, d0 theta1 = -v fbar, d0 theta2 = v u. This is Lemma A (E-240) with R = k^2, X = {bbar, c, fbar, u, v}, X1 = {v}, Y = {fbar, u}, one theta for each composable pair (x then y): v then fbar, v then u are the only ones (v is the only arrow into i; fbar and u the only arrows out of i), X1 and Y disjoint, D = 0 on all arrows. Lemma A gives (kM, d0) -> T(X)/(v fbar, v u) a q.i. (the signs of d0 theta, -1 and +1, are absorbed by rescaling theta1). A map of filtered complexes with finite filtrations that is a q.i. on gr is a q.i. QED. Weakest step: the printed differentials and the sign set (E-202), as in E-290; and that the 'ideal generated by Thetas and their differentials' is Prop. 7.6's N (E-284's reading).
Why this differs from M1 (E-290): there d theta1 contains the generator bbar, so a change of generators works. Here d theta1, d theta2 contain no generator; the pivots are products (v fbar, v u), and the obstruction of E-290 ("Anick lemma needed") is met by choosing the filtration so that the leading parts are disjoint quadratic monomials: a Theta-count filtration is not needed.
