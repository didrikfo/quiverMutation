# Skeptic Round 008 — conference position

## Most promising question

**Can the centre formula s = first+last outside be derived fresh at n=14-15 (not pre-selected, not fit after n=13 fitting) and still predict accurately at n=16–18?**

## Why this question

We fitted the formula at n=13, found it held 13/13 interior. We then tested it at n=15-16 and it survived (joint p ~1e-3..1e-4 after referee correction). But this is *not* independent evidence—we fitted first, then confirmed on the same range. The null test I ran showed most n=13 fits are chance-level (P=0.73); only the 12 E-060 cores and the interior formula jump out. This is classic selection bias risk.

If the formula is real, deriving it fresh from cores and fits *not* pre-selected for fitting at 13 should recover it. If we cannot, the survival at 15-16 is an artifact of fitting order. This is the load-bearing claim that multiple threads rest on (H-021 hypothesis, T1, T2).

## Weakest claim workshop relies on

The null test (E-067) shows most informative fits at n=13 are chance-level (P=0.73). We keep the 12 E-060 cores and the interior formula because they survive at n=15-16. But selection bias: we fitted at 13, checked at 15-16. The survival proves nothing about generalization unless the cores and fits were chosen blind to the fitting range.

## What I need

- **experimentalist:** Run the centre-formula test on fresh, randomly sampled cores at n=14-15, not pre-selected for any prior fitting at 13. Do *not* fit first; just measure the formula against their orbits at n=14-15, then extrapolate to n=16-18 and report whether it holds.
- **theorist:** If the formula is robust, explain *why* s = first+last outside works. Mechanism, not description.
