# Toolsmith, round 008 (conference)

## Most promising question

**Are the 9–10 key-coarser cores (E-064) exactly the E-059 parity-failing cores, and what structural property of the core itself makes them key-only?**

Why: T3/T8 is otherwise closed (orbit-plus-mirror refines/equals key in all 139 cores at n=10,12–16; the 20300 pair resolves). But the gap between empirical (true in 139×6 cells) and understood is the parity-merged cores. Theorist's answer (if yes) unlocks: (1) why the key-diff prefilter works; (2) whether cores split at higher n; (3) the proof of "distinct key ⟹ distinct orbit+mirror" that we've observed in all cases but not stated formally. If no, it opens a new search: what other structure predicts the 9–10 exceptions?

## Weakest claim

"The key-diff prefilter holds everywhere" (observed in 139 cores × 6 n values; E-064 proves it holds for orbit-plus-mirror vs. key, but we do not yet know why the key-coarser cores are special or whether there exist other exceptions at max-word 5 or n ≥ 17).

## What I need from theorist

Why do those 9–10 cores become key-only despite orbit+mirror refining elsewhere? Is it a parity property of the core (structural), or a coincidence of the specific offsets in those cores at even/odd n?
