## Most promising question for the next few rounds
Can a derived invariant that is not a Cartan, Coxeter or key invariant separate two same-key periodic LNAs at n = 12, where the key groups are all Cartan-congruent (E-242) and the growth profile of nu^k P_l does not split them (E-253)?
Why: the shift on projectives (S^a ~ [b], with a, b read off each FCY member, e.g. a = 15, b = -16 at n = 11) is the one structure that already distinguishes the groups, and it is cheap to compute with the bimodule resolution (E-246). If two key-equal groups disagree on (a, b) or on the sign pattern, we get the first real NO on a periodic pair that the Cartan level cannot give.

That the J = 0 premise ("25 of 25 failing n = 7 children joined") is the right premise for the key guard. The only NO on a key-equal pair (n = 9 F-010 pair, E-219/E-226) is one-sided and ran below the radius where YES controls join (E-233, E-234). The 2400230 ~ 3304000 move-set gap (E-250, E-254) is still open, so the NO has no demonstrated power. Every "25 of 25" statement carries this caveat, and the ledger does not yet show a test that could fail.

Second weakest, in my own thread: "the verdict is constant on each key group" (E-253). It rests on 4 full and 4 sampled n = 12 groups, and the growth runs were not independently re-run in 091.
