## Claim

(1) **What is printed.** Prop. 7.6: *(kM, d) is quasi-isomorphic to its dg quotient generated by the arrows phi, alpha phi, alpha^* with relations phi alpha^* = -(-1)^|phi| (d phi)/alpha and alpha phi beta^* = -(-1)^|phi| alpha (d phi)/beta.* Its proof is two sentences: modulo terms psi beta^{-1} with psi of smaller degree, d(phi alpha^{-1}) = (-1)^||phi|| phi alpha^* - (-1)^chi(t phi) (d phi)/alpha, "cancelling the phi alpha^{-1} for their differential" gives the relation, and ||phi|| = |phi| - 1 + chi(t phi) gives the sign; the second relation "is similar". The sign arithmetic is correct (checked for both relations, loop case ||phi|| = |phi|, ||alpha phi|| = ||phi|| - 1). What is **not** printed: any statement of a cancellation lemma, the triangularity, and why the cancelled relations leave no higher syzygies.

(2) **Hypotheses in force.** Exactly Thm 1.1's: Setup l. 282-283 (non-negatively graded dg algebra, free over R = k^n on degree-wise finite generators, k a field) and "i has no loop of degree 0" (l. 264). Nothing on cycles, finite dimension, J, parallel arrows, or loops of degree >= 1 (Step 4 handles them). The no-degree-0-loop hypothesis is what makes every generator of M non-negatively graded (||phi|| = |phi| - 1 >= 0 for phi out of i not in A, since all degree-0 arrows out of i are in A) and every anti-composition of degree >= 1 (||phi alpha^{-1}|| = ||phi|| + 1; ||alpha phi beta^{-1}|| = |phi| >= 1 for a loop phi).

(3) **Does it cover step 7 for acyclic D(i)?** Not by itself, and for End(T) not at all (see Evidence B). Chain for H_0: End(T) = H_0(Gamma) = H_0(Gamma_red) (Thm 7.5, proof printed in full and complete) = H_0(N) (needs Gamma_red = N, unchecked) = H_0(kM) (Evidence B). The link from H_0(kM) to the repo's kernel is E-199 (proved, acyclic D(i)); the link from the repo's (Q, I) to Oppermann's input Q^(<=2) is Constr. 2.2 plus E-189's derivation, not the paper. (4) **Does it exclude E-211?** No. E-211's parent is a finite-dimensional algebra of finite global dimension, so Constr. 2.2 gives a degree-wise finite Q, i = 1 carries no loop, and Thm 1.1 applies with the cycle 3 <-> 4 meeting D(1). ## Evidence

**A. Triangularity, for all terms of d(phi alpha^{-1}), including the Delta-expanded ones (completes E-196's open remark).** V := arrows phi, alpha phi, alpha^*; X := phi alpha^{-1}, alpha phi beta^{-1}. All generators have degree >= 0 (above) and d lowers degree by 1, so every term of d(x) is a product of generators each of degree <= deg x - 1. Hence d(X_p) lies in the subalgebra B_{p-1} generated by V_{<=p-1} and X_{<=p-1} (no letter of degree p can occur), and likewise d(V_p) in B_{p-1}. So kM is a union of dg subalgebras B_p = B_{p-1} * T(V_p + X_p), semi-free with cells attached in increasing degree. Delta = 1 - sum alpha^{-1} alpha only inserts letters of degree <= deg x - 1 (they sit inside a term of total degree deg x - 1). Uses: non-negative grading only.

**B. End(T) = H_0(kM) needs no cancellation.** Let J be the two-sided ideal generated by X and d(X); it is a dg ideal (d^2 = 0), N := kM/J. A degree-0 element of J is a sum of u y v, y in X or d(X), u, v paths; deg X >= 1 and the grading is >= 0, so y = d(x) with deg x = 1 and deg u = deg v = 0, and then u d(x) v = d(u x v) because d vanishes on degree 0 (nothing in degree -1). So J_0 is contained in d(kM_1), hence H_0(N) = kM_0 / (J_0 + d kM_1) = kM_0 / d kM_1 = H_0(kM). Uses: non-negative grading and deg X >= 1 only (i.e. no degree-0 loop at i). H_1 and higher are exactly what Prop. 7.6 is for: J, hence tilting vs silting, is not decided by this (consistent with E-181, E-189).

**C. The gap in (1), made precise (my completion, sketch).** Modulo (X), d(phi alpha^{-1}) = +-(phi alpha^* - rho) with rho = +-(d phi)/alpha, because every term of d(phi) alpha^{-1} lies in (X) and Delta -> 1. rho is a combination of paths in Q-arrows and composites alpha phi, with **no letter alpha^***. Order words so that words containing alpha^* are larger; then the leading word of the relation is phi alpha^* (or (alpha phi) beta^*), a word of the form (letter u)(letter alpha^*) with u an arrow out of i that is a phi or an alpha phi. Two leading words overlap iff the right letter of one is the left letter of another; alpha^* is never a phi or an alpha phi, so there are **no overlaps** (self-overlaps included), and the rho_x are a Groebner basis, with N = T(V)/(rho). In Anick's language this is a strongly free (inert) set (Anick 1982, from memory, UNVERIFIED), and T(V + X, d x = rho_x) -> T(V)/(rho) is a quasi-isomorphism. The induction on B_p is meant to carry this to the dg setting. The step I have not done: the dg version (V carries a nonzero d; the cells X_p attach to B_{p-1}, not to N_{p-1}), i.e. that inertness survives passing from B_{p-1} to H(B_{p-1}); I believe it, did not prove it. This is where the printed proof is a sketch.

**D. What is not available from Prop. 7.6.** The paper's proof of Thm 1.1 is Gamma ~ Gamma_red (7.5) = N (list l. 1015-1022; the differential of hat-arrows, Lemma 7.4 and l. 921, matches d mod J but is not displayed as a dg isomorphism) ~ kM (7.6). The quasi-isomorphisms in H_1 and above, hence the claim that kM is REnd(T), rest on C and on N = Gamma_red. The theorem as a theorem is therefore proved at H_0 (B) and sketched above it.
