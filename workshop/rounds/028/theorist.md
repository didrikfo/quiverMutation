# Theorist position statement (Round 028)

## Most promising question

**Why do LNA-derived algebras never have circuits of length > 2 in their circuit graph?**

This cuts to the heart of W: if all circuits on LNA-derived algebras are length-2 ground paths (as E-112/E-113 suggest), then W is not just a condition on the kernel but a necessary consequence of the mutation rule itself. E-112 proved this holds for walks; E-113 shows 0 rejects at out-degree >= 3. The next step is to ask whether the mutation rule forbids longer circuits, and if so, why. This would settle whether the converse (reject => W) is provable from step 7 or forever empirical.

## Weakest claim

The workshop relies on W being necessary and sufficient for rejection, but the converse rests empirically on 70 same-shape rejects with no proof. The "cancels" branch (hand-built D from E-105) is never exercised by the experimental data. The claim that "J != 0 iff circuit graph has circuit" lacks the balanced-circuit lemma for scalars != 1 and evidence on >= 3-term relations. Until these are filled, W is a strong empirical pattern, not a theorem.

## What I need

From experimentalist: hand-built parallel-arrow and "cancels" controls for W (the D algebra); balanced-circuit examples with scalar != 1; evidence on >= 3-term relations. From scholar: what forces the circuit graph in W to be the mutation rule's invariant, or a proof that it is not (a proof-shaped question).
