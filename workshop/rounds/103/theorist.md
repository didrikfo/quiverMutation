## Claim

(1) [Narrowed after review: for a model of A built by Construction 2.2 (A finite-dimensional, basic), a loop of degree >= 1 at i implies i on an oriented cycle of Q0 (proof below; machine-checked, see Response 1). Hence the library at one step never reaches Step 4 for the algebra it holds. Chains through H_0 (E-249) are not covered.] For the library, the case "gate-admitted step with a loop of degree >= 1 at i" cannot be reached through `mutateAtVertex` (default) or the search. In a dg model of A built by Construction 2.2, a loop of any degree >= 1 at i forces an oriented closed path of A's ordinary quiver through i, hence i on an oriented cycle, hence D(i) cyclic, which `mutateAtVertex` refuses (`CyclicDescendantsError`) and the search never expands (`noCycles`). So Step 4 of Def. 3.2 (the arrows `alpha phi beta^-1`) is empty for every step the library takes, and E-235's hypothesis "no loop at i of any degree" holds there automatically. (2) None of the three cited theorems has a hypothesis about loops of degree >= 1 or cyclic D(i): Oppermann Thm 1.1 excludes only degree-0 loops; Aihara-Iyama Thm 2.31/2.32 and Rickard (Keller, Thm 3.12) speak only about Hom vanishing. (3) The one hypothesis that fails for a loop phi of degree >= 1 is Lemma A's "D = 0 on spectators and D theta_xy = xy" (E-240): d_1 of the cancelling letter `phi alpha^-1` is `+-phi alpha* + sum_beta +-beta*(beta phi alpha^-1)`, not a monomial. That is a gap in our proof of Lemma A, not in Oppermann's Prop. 7.6 as printed. It would be refuted, for the library, by a gate-admitted J = 0 step whose parent has i on a cycle and whose `allowCyclic=True` child is not derived equivalent; the library cannot produce one that is reliable (E-277: 394 of 495 and 401 of 491 such calls in the two sweeps are a `KeyError`; the rest are unproved and use vertex-simple paths, E-193).

Which hypothesis of what holds for a step with a loop phi of degree m >= 1 at i (A = degree-0 arrows out of i, none a loop):
- Oppermann Thm 1.1 / 7.5 / 7.6: holds (only degree-0 loops excluded).
- Aihara-Iyama 2.31, 2.32; Keller 3.12: no loop or cycle hypothesis; the only inputs are `Hom(T, T[<>0])`, covariant finiteness (automatic: finitely many summands, Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt) and generation (2.31). J = 0 is the 2.32 / 3.12 input, but the repo computes J and Cartan by vertex-simple path enumeration, which is not valid with an oriented cycle (E-193, `warnIfCyclic`), so on such inputs "gate-admitted J = 0" is itself unreliable.
- Lemma A (E-240): X1 = arrows leaving i (here including `phi` and `beta phi`), Y = {alpha*}; X1 and Y stay disjoint (a loop is a different letter from alpha*), so the skeleton argument's step (1) survives. What fails is "D theta = xy, D = 0 on spectators": from Def. 3.2, `d_1(phi alpha^-1) = (-1)^||phi|| phi alpha* + sum_beta beta* (beta phi alpha^-1) + lower terms` (the middle term is `partial(phi) alpha^-1`, `partial(phi) ∋ sum beta* beta phi` for `t(phi) = i`, l. 386). E-235's control (6 968 of 10 206 loop letters) is this.

Proof of (1). Let Q0 be the quiver of A, acyclic or not. Claim: every arrow phi: a -> b of degree m >= 1 in a model from Construction 2.2 has a path of degree-0 arrows of length >= 1 from a to b in Q0. m = 1: d phi is a nonzero combination of degree-0 paths from a to b (a relation). m >= 2: d phi is a nonzero cycle in degree m-1 >= 1, a combination of paths a -> b each made of arrows of smaller degree, each with such a path by induction; concatenate. Hence a loop at i of degree >= 1 gives a closed path of length >= 1 at i in Q0, a shortest one is a simple oriented cycle through i, so `nx.descendants(Q, i) | {i}` induces a cyclic subgraph. Weakest step: "d phi is nonzero" (true for Construction 2.2 generators; fails for a non-minimal model with free generators, which Thm 1.1 also covers but the library never builds). The converse is not claimed: i on a cycle does not imply a loop in the model.

Library check. (a) E-211 parent (a:1->2, s:2->3, b:3->4, c:4->3; as, sbc, cb): i = 1 is off the cycle, D(1) cyclic: gate True, refused by default, `allowCyclic=True` returns (E-211's failure is step 7, not a loop: no loop at 1, Step 4 empty). i = 3 and 4 (on the cycle): `isMutable` False; `allowCyclic=True` is `KeyError: (0, (2, 3, 0))` and `(0, (3, 4, 0))`. (b) Sweep (seed 1 and 2, 300 algebras each): gate True with i on a cycle: 495 and 491; default call refuses all of them; `allowCyclic=True` is `KeyError` in 394 and 401, returns in 101 and 90. Off cycle (a cycle elsewhere in D(i)): 160 and 181 gate-true, default refuses 145 and 161, returns 15 and 20 (D(i) acyclic, covered by E-199). So the default path never meets a loop at i.

What a counterexample would look like. Type (a), repo rule differs from Oppermann's: a parent with i on a cycle where `reduce(mutateAtVertex(..., allowCyclic=True))` has a Cartan or Coxeter polynomial (true paths) different from the tilting prediction `End(T)`, as E-211 did for a cycle in D(i) off i. Type (b), Prop. 7.6 itself fails: J = 0 T from an A with a degree-m loop at i whose true End(T) is not the quotient of M; that would contradict printed Prop. 7.6, so it needs an independent Hom computation in K^b(proj A) (E-238 style). Neither exists in the record. Weakest part of the whole picture: the loop case of Prop. 7.6 rests on a two-sentence proof; a two-stage filtration (filter by the number of Step-3 letters `phi alpha^-1`, which `d_1`, `beta* theta'` and `phi alpha*` all lower; first kill the Step-4 letters by Lemma A on the graded piece, then the Step-3 letters on E^1) looks workable but is not carried out and not tested.
