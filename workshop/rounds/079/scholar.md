## Evidence

The one point not already in E-214/E-220: *shape of the proposed leading words*. The relations of 7.6 have the form (leading) = (rest) with leading words
  W1 = phi alpha^*  (phi a non-star arrow, alpha^* a star arrow) and W2 = (alpha phi) beta^*  (non-star arrow of type "alpha phi", then a star arrow).
Every leading word is (non-star)(star). An overlap of two leading words (a proper suffix of one equal to a proper prefix of another, or a self-overlap) would need a star letter beta^* to be the first letter of some leading word; none is. So the set {W1, W2} has no overlaps, in the combinatorial sense used for Anick-type resolutions. This is a hand argument on the printed relations, the star arrows, the phi's and the alpha phi's being distinct generators of kM (checked, Steps 1-4 of Sect. "mutation", l. 315-360; see Response 2).

What this does *not* give: (i) that W1, W2 are leading terms for any monomial order for which "the rest" is smaller, since the paper filters by the degree of the cancelled generators, not by words; the "rest" d phi/alpha contains words in non-star arrows, so an order must put phi alpha^* above them; (ii) that the ideal generated by x = phi alpha^{-1} and partial(x) is such that kM -> kM/(x, partial x) is a quasi-isomorphism (for a tensor algebra this is the statement that the elements partial(x) form a strongly free / inert set; Anick or Dicks, UNVERIFIED, from memory, not read). The printed proof is a cancellation of a contractible pair in the sense of the paper's earlier Sect. 5-6 (l. 630, 861), which are the same device for a different pair; whether those are proved in the paper, I did not check beyond grep.

Sign arithmetic: ||phi|| = |phi| - 1 + c(t(phi)) turns (-1)^{||phi||} phi alpha^* - (-1)^{c(t phi)} d phi/alpha into phi alpha^* = -(-1)^{|phi|} d phi/alpha: matches the printed relation (E-214 had checked this; I rechecked the one line).
