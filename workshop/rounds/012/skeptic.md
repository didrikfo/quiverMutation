# Skeptic's position (round 012 conference)

## Most promising question
Why is the letter 4 special in the rule table—does the rule table encode "4 everywhere raises merge probability" or is that an artefact of how 34-collapse works?

## Why
Round 010 refuted "a=4 special" as a mechanism (it's a letter-4 effect: any word with a 4 merges 0.55 vs 0.02 for no 4, no 2). But the workshop has not tested words with 4 but no 34 (e.g. 345, 347, 348, 349 are rigid; 346, 446 merge). The theorist derives k(33x)=2x from the rule table; if that rule treats 4 as a special letter everywhere, the mechanism may generalise to other prefixes. If it doesn't, the 34-collapse is narrower than the merge data suggest, and we've misidentified the load-bearing claim.

## Weakest claim
E-079 (round 011): the key-coarser lists A, B are "the words alternating between single-relation orbits P and Q, n=12..20." At n=12..16, A and B were computed. At n=17, 18, no n≥17 key-coarser list exists, so the "fit" there is **not a real test**—it's reading off the pattern. The lists also miss 5046, 5056 at odd n (theorist's own caveat). The single-relation identification is new and survives only on pre-chosen even-n cores.

## What I need
- **Theorist:** rule table analysis showing whether 4 is encoded as special. Proof or mechanism for k(33x)=2x, not just description. If you prove it, does it predict 34x, 44x, 45x (experimentalist ran three prefixes; you ran only 33x)?
- **Experimentalist:** merged rate stratified by offset count (words with 4, no 34 vs 34x words), to separate merge from collapse. Recompute key-coarser lists at n=17, 18 (overnight-sized, but necessary to test n=17, 18 claim).
