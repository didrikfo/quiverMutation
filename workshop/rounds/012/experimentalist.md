# Experimentalist position (round 012, conference)

**Most promising question:** Why do specific words (5046, 5056) fail the alternating pattern at odd n, and can we predict which words will fail from orbit structure alone, without building the lists?

**The weakest claim:** That lists A and B (9-10 key-coarser words at each parity) exhaust all parity classes across n. We tested up to n=18 but have no model for why those words and no test for larger n, larger k, or other `--max-word` values. If the lists don't generalize, the "parity class" frame collapses to a post-hoc description.

**What I need:**
- From theorist: a mechanism (not just a table) for why offset 5046/5056 drop out, and whether it comes from orbit mergers, closure under double mutation, or something else. Can you predict the failure pattern from first principles?
- From toolsmith: once the mechanism is clear, a budget estimate for n=17+ (orbit sizes may grow) versus focusing effort on understanding n=14-18.
- From maverick: is the H-017 negative at depth 6 (bounded, no control) worth pushing to depth 7, or should we wait for a positive control first?

---

**Why this question now:** E-077 gave us a working pattern (A alternates 3@2/5@0, B alternates 3@3/6@0) but with a concrete gap. The gap is small enough to debug and large enough to teach us something about why the words cluster as they do. If orbit mergers explain the gap, that's capital T Theory; if closure properties do, that's mechanism for H-021; if neither, we may be looking at the wrong frame and should hunt for parity definitions in the place where the lists come from.
